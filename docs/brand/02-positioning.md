# 02 — Positioning

*Why the right client chooses this studio over every alternative. Nothing else belongs here.*
*Version 2 · 3 August 2026 · governed by `01-vision.md`*

---

## 1. The sentence

> **Digital experiences built for moments that only happen once.**

Used verbatim. Two words carry it: *experience*, not site — *moment*, not client or brand.

*Only happen once* is a test a client applies to themselves. We are not deciding which moments deserve attention; we are describing which ones we work on.

## 2. The category

There is no established category for this, and we should not borrow one. We are not a web studio, not a design agency, not a wedding vendor. Each of those is named after an output. We are defined by an input — the kind of occasion we accept.

**Everyone else is organised around what they make. We are organised around what happens once.**

That is the whole of the difference, and it is not adoptable. A studio that copied it would have to decline most of its revenue on Monday morning.

## 3. Who we are for

One question decides it:

> **Does emotion matter more than information?**

In practice, three things have to be true:

1. **The moment is singular.** It happens once, or once a season. It will not be repeated identically.
2. **Feeling is the outcome.** What the client cares about is how people arrive and what they carry away.
3. **There is material.** A place, a person, a body of work — something real to build from.

| Where this happens | The moment |
|---|---|
| Weddings | one day, planned for a year, attended once |
| Exhibitions and openings | a show that exists for six weeks |
| Artists and authors | a body of work, a release, a residency |
| Cultural launches | a season, a festival, a film, a record |
| Private events | an anniversary, a dinner, a gathering |
| Hospitality | a hotel, a restaurant, a house opening |

This describes where the work has come from. It is not the definition and it is not a menu — the three tests are. A field we have never worked in qualifies the moment it passes them.

## 4. Who we are not for

- Anyone whose product lives in a daily routine: apps, tools, dashboards, catalogues.
- Anyone whose goal is throughput — more visitors, more orders, more leads.
- Anyone who has already decided what should be built and needs it executed.
- Anyone for whom the date matters more than the result. Attention takes time and does not compress.
- Anyone who wants a website.

The last one matters most. Someone who wants a website will be better served and considerably happier elsewhere, and saying so in the first ten minutes is part of the work.

## 5. The decision they are really making

They believe they are choosing a supplier. They are choosing between two ways of treating the same occasion.

The first treats it as a task with a known answer: gather the information, publish it, be correct. It is fast, cheap, entirely reasonable, and it is what nearly everyone does.

The second treats it as something that happens once and afterwards exists only in what was made of it.

Whoever chooses the second is buying three things, and none of them is a website:

1. **A decision about what the moment means** — made explicit, and agreed before anything is designed.
2. **A designed arrival** — the first seconds, and the pace of everything after them.
3. **Something that outlives the day** — the thing people go back to when it is over.

And they are accepting one uncomfortable condition: **the work is made for the people who arrive, not for the person paying.** They are rarely the same person. When the two want different things, we argue for whoever will be standing there. Clients who find that unreasonable are not wrong — they are simply not ours, and it is better to find out in the first conversation.

## 6. Why they choose us

| What they also consider | Why it makes sense | What it costs them |
|---|---|---|
| A template platform | Immediate, cheap, correct | The occasion gets a container built for a thousand other occasions |
| A generalist agency | Process, scale, reliability | It is run as a campaign and judged on numbers that have nothing to do with the day |
| A freelancer or a friend | Personal, affordable | No structure; the risk lands on the one date that cannot move |
| A very good design studio | Genuinely excellent work | They are excellent at many things; nobody there has spent years on this one problem |
| Nothing — social media only | Honest, no effort | Nothing survives it; the moment never has a place of its own |

Three sentences a competitor cannot say without changing their business:

- **We only work on things that happen once.**
- **We are responsible for how it feels, not for delivering what was asked for.**
- **The person arriving outranks the person paying, and we say so before we start.**

## 7. What we refuse

Not kinds of work — `01-vision.md` covers that. These are terms, and they hold even for a client we want.

- **Competitive pitches.** We do not design against other studios for free. The work depends on understanding the moment, and nobody understands it during a pitch.
- **Speculative concepts.** Ideas offered before the first real conversation are guesses, and guessing is how defaults get chosen.
- **Discounts taken out of the attention.** The price can move. What is removed can never be the care.
- **Work we are not allowed to argue about.** If we cannot advocate for the person arriving, we are decorating, and a decorator costs less.
- **Repeating ourselves.** What we make for one client is never offered to another.

## 8. What proof matters

In order of weight:

1. **The work, experienced.** Someone should get a piece of the actual thing, not a picture of it. A screenshot proves we can arrange a layout. It cannot prove the only quality we sell.
2. **Our own work, held to the same standard.** We cannot claim to make memorable things from a forgettable place.
3. **How precisely we describe a moment we have not made yet.** In a first conversation, specificity is the one credential that cannot be faked.

The strongest proof travels by itself: someone experiences a project we made for a stranger, and comes looking for who made it.

**What is not proof:** client logos, awards, years in business, volume, testimonials about being easy to work with, and every metric ever put in a case study.
