# 05 — Storyboard

**Version 3 · foundation document 5 of 5**
**Date:** 4 August 2026
**Status:** Draft for approval
**Governed by:** `01-vision.md`, `02-positioning.md`, `03-design-principles.md`, `04-visual-language.md`

The first four documents say why we exist, who we are for, how we think and how we see. This one is the bridge: it describes how a person moves through an experience emotionally, and what has to be true at each point for them to keep going.

It has two halves, and the difference between them matters.

**Part One is the blueprint.** A sequence of human states that applies to any experience we make — the studio's own homepage, a wedding, an artist's release, an exhibition. It should not need rewriting.

**Part Two is the current homepage interpretation.** One implementation of the blueprint, with its counts, its order and its specific decisions. It will be replaced. When it is, Part One does not change.

If the two ever conflict, Part One is right and the implementation is wrong.

# Part One — The blueprint

## 1. Who arrives

Three arrival states. They exist in every experience we will ever build, whatever it is for.

| Arrival | State | Needs in the first ten seconds |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Knows nothing | Atmosphere first. To feel that this is not an ordinary thing before being told anything at all. |
| **Referred** — sent by someone they already trust | Already warm | Confirmation that the referral was right, and a way to begin. |
| **Downstream** — arrived through the work itself | Convinced | Only to find us, and to understand that what they just experienced was made here. |

→ *The experience is built for the cold visitor.* The other two need a fast route past the atmosphere, not a different experience: any moment must be skippable, and the way to act must be reachable at any point.

Building for the warm visitor is the more comfortable mistake, because the warm visitor is the one we talk to. They are not the one who decides whether this works.

## 2. The three tests

Applied to anything we make, at any scale.

- **Five seconds.** *What kind of thing is this?* Answered by atmosphere alone, with no explanation available.
- **Thirty seconds.** *What is this, and is it for me?* Answered by one assertion and one piece of real evidence.
- **Two minutes.** *Why them, and what do I do?* Answered by understanding how the work happens, and by a way to act.

Anything that fails the five-second test is not saved by what comes after it. There is no recovery, because there is no audience left to recover.

## 3. The emotional progression

Seven states. They are not sections, scenes or screens. They are what is happening inside one person, in order.

Each has a question the person is actually asking, the thing that moves them to the next state, and the way it fails.

### Arrival — *Where am I?*

Nothing has been read yet. This is pre-verbal: the person is deciding whether this deserves another second, and deciding it from atmosphere rather than information.

**Moves on when:** they sense the place was made deliberately, by someone, for a reason.
**Fails when:** we hand them information before they have anywhere to put it.

### Orientation — *What kind of thing is this?*

Now they want a frame. Not detail — category. Without it, everything that follows is unplaceable, and they leave holding nothing even if they stayed a while.

**Moves on when:** one assertion lands, unmissably, and it costs something to say.
**Fails when:** we mistake orientation for persuasion and begin arguing before they know what we are.

### Curiosity — *Show me.*

The first moment they want something rather than tolerate it. It is a small opening and it goes off quickly.

**Moves on when:** they are given something real and specific, immediately.
**Fails when:** we spend the opening on ourselves — history, philosophy, credentials — instead of the work.

### Recognition — *Is this for me?*

The hinge of the whole sequence. Until someone sees their own situation in front of them, nothing said about quality registers, because it is being said about somebody else.

Recognition is not being told *this is for you*. It is seeing one specific thing they know from their own life.

**Moves on when:** something is specific enough to be recognised.
**Fails when:** we offer breadth. A list of everyone we serve tells nobody they are on it.

### Trust — *Can they do this, and can I hand it over?*

Two separate questions: capability, and safety. Trust is inferred and never asserted. It accumulates from evidence, from a plainly described method, and from the absence of overclaiming.

**Moves on when:** they understand how the work actually happens and what their own part in it is.
**Fails when:** we claim it. Praise, awards and adjectives are what gets offered instead of trust, and they read as a substitute.

### Commitment — *What do I do, and what happens if I do?*

Nobody acts into uncertainty. The size of the step being asked for has to match the trust that has actually been earned by this point — asking for more is where sequences break, and it usually happens because we counted the trust we intended rather than the trust we produced.

**Moves on when:** they know exactly what happens next, and have been told honestly who this is not for.
**Fails when:** the step is larger than the trust supports, or its cost is hidden.

### Departure — *What do I keep?*

Most people leave without acting. That is the normal outcome, not the failure case, and what they carry away is the real product of the sequence — it is what gets repeated to somebody else later, which is how most work actually travels.

**Succeeds when:** one thing stays with them — an image, a sentence, a feeling — and the way back is obvious.
**Fails when:** we treat leaving as a loss and try to prevent it. Anything that detains someone at the exit converts the whole sequence into an interruption retrospectively.

## 4. How the progression behaves

Seven rules. They govern every implementation.

1. **The order is fixed. The duration is not.** Nobody arrives at trust before recognition. But how long any state takes belongs to the person, always — we decide the order, they decide the pace.
2. **Only the person may skip. We may not omit.** Any state can be jumped over by someone in a hurry. None can be left out by us — an omitted state does not get skipped, it gets failed silently.
3. **No state is reached by being told it has been reached.** You cannot instruct someone into recognition, or announce trust into existence. Each one is a conclusion the person draws, and our only move is to make it available.
4. **Nothing is banked.** Progress is not cumulative. One friction, one overclaim, one thing that does not answer, and the person is back at Arrival or gone. Trust behaves like this most sharply.
5. **Recognition is the hinge.** Everything before it earns attention. Everything after it spends attention. Work that fails tends to fail here, and then blame a later state for the loss.
6. **Departure is a state we design,** not an outcome we absorb.
7. **States are not moments.** One moment can carry two states; one state can need three moments. How many moments an implementation uses, and where its edges fall, is an implementation decision — always. The blueprint never specifies a count.

**The one question,** asked of any moment in anything we build: *which state is this serving, and what does the person believe after it?* If the answer is two states, it is doing two jobs. If the answer is none, it is decoration.

## 5. The blueprint applied

The same seven states, in four different kinds of work. This is the test of whether the progression is universal: if a column cannot be filled in, the blueprint is too narrow and the blueprint is what changes.

| State | Studio homepage | A wedding experience | An artist's release | An exhibition or installation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arrival** | The room | The light of the day itself | A sound or an image, before any name | The threshold, before the first work |
| **Orientation** | What we do, and for whom | Whose day this is, and when | What this is — a record, a film, a live thing | What this show is about |
| **Curiosity** | A fragment of a real project | One image of the two of them | One track, one frame | The first piece, chosen to open |
| **Recognition** | A moment from their own domain | The guest finding their own part in the day | The listener hearing their own year in it | The visitor finding their own reference |
| **Trust** | How the work happens | The details are handled — travel, time, what to wear | The work is finished, and complete | The curation holds; nothing here is arbitrary |
| **Commitment** | Writing to us, and the filter on it | Replying, and arriving | Listening all the way through | Going further in, or coming back |
| **Departure** | The last frame | What they tell someone tomorrow | The one track they replay | What they carry out of the building |

Note what changes between columns and what does not. The states hold. The moments that carry them are entirely different, and so is the pacing — a wedding experience has a fixed audience who will return to it repeatedly, an installation has one pass and no scroll. The blueprint does not care.

## 6. Conditions on every implementation

True regardless of medium, format or year.

**The first frame is part of the story.** Nobody watches a thing assemble itself. Arrival begins with the first thing on screen, which means the first thing on screen is composed, not whatever loaded first.

**Composed for what it will actually meet.** Not scaled to it. A moment on a phone held vertically outdoors is a different composition of the same state, made separately — never the wide version reflowed and hoped for.

**Every version is designed.** Reduced motion, no scripting, a slow connection, a bright afternoon. Each of these is a composed alternative carrying the same meaning, never the same thing with parts missing. `03-design-principles.md` sets this as a condition; here it means the progression must survive in all of them, five-second test included.

**Delivery is a narrative constraint.** The experience holds its frame rate on ordinary hardware in ordinary conditions. An idea that cannot be delivered reliably is not a good idea awaiting optimisation — it is the wrong idea, and it is replaced rather than repaired.

# Part Two — Current homepage interpretation

*This is one implementation of Part One, for one page, at this moment. Its counts, its order and its specifics are choices — they will change, possibly completely, and nothing above this line changes with them.*

*Everything here has to be traceable to a state in §3. Anything that cannot be traced is not a beat; it is something we wanted to include.*

## 7. The shape

Six beats and an exit. Roughly two minutes end to end, legible in twenty seconds if scrolled straight through.

| # | Beat | Serves | Purpose | What they know after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Load** | Arrival | Arrive somewhere, not into a blank page | Nothing yet. The room already exists. |
| 1 | **The room** | Arrival | Establish atmosphere and category without explaining | Something is being shown to me. This was made deliberately. |
| 2 | **The claim** | Orientation | State the position, unmissably | What we do, and roughly for whom. |
| 3 | **The evidence** | Curiosity, and the start of Trust | Show one project as an experience | They can actually do this. |
| 4 | **The range** | Recognition | Prove the studio is not one domain | This applies to my kind of moment. |
| 5 | **The method** | Trust | Explain why the work is possible | Why so few projects, and what my part is. |
| 6 | **The invitation** | Commitment | Ask, and filter | Whether I qualify, and exactly what happens if I write. |
| — | **The exit** | Departure | Leave something behind | What this studio was, in one image. |

Two things worth noticing about this mapping, because they are what make beats and states different.

Beat 3 carries a state and a half — it satisfies Curiosity and begins Trust, which is why it does more work than any other beat and is the hardest to make. And Trust is spread across two beats, because on a homepage capability and safety are answered by different things: evidence answers *can they*, method answers *can I hand it over*. Another implementation could merge them. A wedding experience almost certainly would.

## 8. The beats

### Beat 0 — Load

The visitor never sees a blank page filling in. The ground is present in the first frame; one element is there before anything else arrives.

**Failure mode:** a white flash, a spinner, or a layout that shifts once fonts land.

### Beat 1 — The room

One subject in an environment. Almost no words. The atmosphere does the work a headline usually does badly.

**Must not:** explain, greet, or offer a menu of options. No navigation competing for the first look. No prompt telling the visitor to scroll.
**Motion budget:** one thing moving, slowly, or nothing moving at all. Stillness is valid here and is probably the braver choice.
**Failure mode:** it reads as a hero image. If it could hold a button in the middle, it is a hero image.

### Beat 2 — The claim

The positioning sentence, alone on the screen, at a size that makes it the only thing there.

Immediately after it, one line naming the kinds of moments — enough that a gallerist does not leave believing this is a wedding site.

**Must not:** be softened by a subheading, a button, or a second sentence explaining the first.
**Failure mode:** the visitor reads it and thinks *of course you do*. The sentence has to cost something to say.

### Beat 3 — The evidence

One project. Not a grid, not a carousel. The visitor gets a **fragment of the actual experience** — a real moment from it, behaving the way it behaves — rather than a picture of a screen.

**Consequence for production:** our first case piece must be built so that it can be excerpted. That is a construction requirement, decided before it is made, not a crop chosen afterwards.
**Must not:** show a laptop, a phone frame, or a scrolling screenshot.
**Failure mode:** it looks like a portfolio entry. Then it is a portfolio entry.

### Beat 4 — The range

Three or four moments from different domains. One image and one line each — the specific detail of that project, not the category it belongs to.

**Must not:** become a services grid, acquire icons, or list six domains for completeness.
**Failure mode:** the lines are interchangeable. If a line could be moved to another item without anyone noticing, it is a label, not a line.

### Beat 5 — The method

Three moves, plainly stated: how we start, what we do, what the client does. This is where the small number of projects is explained as a consequence of the method rather than announced as a boast.

**Must not:** become a numbered process diagram with arrows.
**Failure mode:** it describes an agency. Everything here should only be true of a studio that takes very few projects.

### Beat 6 — The invitation

An ask, with a qualifier attached. The qualifier is the intake filter from `02-positioning.md` §3 written as a human sentence — it should make some people not write, on purpose.

The visitor is told exactly what happens next: who replies, how fast, and what the first conversation is. No form with twelve fields. It asks about the moment, not about the website.

**Failure mode:** a contact section that could be lifted onto any site. If it is a headline and a button, the whole page has just ended in a template.

### The exit

If the visitor does nothing, what remains on screen should be worth stopping on. The last thing they see is not a footer of links but the end of a sequence, with the footer beneath it doing its plain job quietly.

## 9. Mobile

Most visitors arrive on a phone, held vertically, possibly outdoors.

- Beats are re-composed, not reflowed. A wide frame becomes a tall one, composed separately.
- The pacing changes: the same story in fewer screens, because a phone screen holds one thought comfortably and no more.
- Anything depending on hover has a designed equivalent, not a fallback.
- Every beat is judged on a phone in daylight before it is judged anywhere else.

## 10. The other versions

Three variants, designed as part of the work rather than derived from it:

- **Reduced motion.** The same beats, the same meaning, arriving without travel.
- **No JavaScript.** The story is legible in full, in order.
- **Slow connection.** The atmosphere degrades to type and ground — and type and ground alone still have to pass the five-second test.

## 11. Performance envelope

Set as a design constraint, before the design exists.

- The first frame arrives fast enough that Beat 0 is a beat and not a wait.
- The opening holds a steady frame rate on a mid-range phone three years old.
- Media never delays the first meaningful thing on screen.

## 12. What must exist before this can be built

1. Answers to `open-decisions.md` — chiefly the name, the two typefaces, and whether dark or light is the studio's resting state.
2. One project that qualifies as Beat 3. If none exists, it is made, and we say openly that it was.
3. Real images for beats 1, 3 and 4. The design is not judged with placeholders — placeholder-driven layouts are how sites end up looking like templates.

## 13. Open decisions on this interpretation

1. **Beat order 3 → 4 → 5.** Evidence before range assumes depth convinces faster than breadth. If the studio leads with weddings (`open-decisions.md` §5), Recognition may need to be served before Curiosity, which reverses these beats.
2. **Does the homepage carry navigation at all,** or is it a single sequence with the rest of the site reached from the end?
3. **How much of the studio appears** — a name only, or a person, and at which beat.
4. **Whether six beats is the right number.** Beat 3 carries a state and a half, and Trust is split across two beats. Both are defensible and neither is required by Part One.
