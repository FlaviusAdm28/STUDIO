## Beat 2 — the claim

> Digital experiences built for moments that only happen once.

Naming line, so a gallerist does not leave believing this is a wedding site:

> A wedding. An exhibition. A record. An opening. A season.

## Beat 5 — the method

**How we start** — With the occasion, not the website. We agree what the moment means and what
someone should still have of it afterwards, and we write that down before anything is designed.

**What we do** — We decide the order things happen in, and how long each one is allowed to take.
That is most of the work, and it is the part that cannot be added at the end.

**What you do** — You give us the real thing — the place, the people, the work, and time with
them. We do not build these from a brief and a folder of files.

Consequence, stated once:

> Which is why we take very few projects a year. Attention does not divide.

## Beat 6 — the invitation

Ask:

> Is there something that only happens once?

Qualifies:

> Write to us if it happens once, if how it feels matters more than what it says, and if there is
> something real to build from — a place, a person, a body of work.

Disqualifies, and it is meant to cost us work:

> Not if the date matters more than the result. We will say so in the first ten minutes, and you
> will be better served elsewhere.

What happens next:

> We reply within two days. The first conversation is about the occasion — where it is, who is
> coming, what you want them to remember. It is not about pages.

## The exit

> What happens once deserves more than information.

## Removed from About — the essay

Entry 54's first pass wrote About as an essay: `01-vision.md`'s structural claim as a display statement,
five labelled passages, a pulled line, and the vision's closing line. Replaced on review by a photograph
and ninety-five words. Kept because three of these passages are the studio's own beliefs restated for a
reader, and because the closing line may find a home somewhere else.

> The world is built for what happens often. We work on what happens once.

> **How it began** — I had spent years making things for the parts of life that repeat — pages somebody
> opens on a Tuesday and again on a Wednesday. That work is worth doing, and none of it is meant to be
> remembered.

> **Why it exists** — An afternoon does not become something people keep on its own. Something has to hold
> it: a photograph, an object, the way a room was arranged. Someone has to decide, a year ahead, what will
> matter afterwards. Screens are the last part of that anyone decides, and the first part anyone sees.

> **What we believe** — Feeling has structure. It has sequence, weight and silence, and it is built or it
> is missing — it cannot be added at the end. People keep details, not summaries. One detail, exactly
> right, does more than everything around it. Restraint is the technique. Anyone can add; the work is in
> what is taken away, and in what is allowed to stay still.

> **Who is behind it** — One person, and whoever a particular occasion actually needs. Not a department.
> The number of projects we take in a year is the number two hands can hold, which is a limit rather than
> a position.

> **What we want to make** — Something a person opens again years afterwards, for no reason except that
> they wanted to. Made once, for one occasion, and still standing long after the occasion is over.

> This has never been anyone's job. We made it ours.

## Removed from the publication — the method as four moves

Entry 55 rebuilt the method as a held frame the visitor goes through rather than a description they read.
These four moves were `05-storyboard.md` §8 Beat 5 delivered as written — *three moves, plainly stated: how
we start, what we do, what the client does* — with a fourth added because the positioning demands it.

Kept because every line of it is true and traceable, and because the fourth is the studio's strongest
sentence about how it works: no competitor can say it without changing their business. If the practical
answers are ever wanted in words again, they belong as rows in the questions rather than as a section.

> **The work is decided / before it is designed.**

> **How we start** — With the occasion, not the website. We agree what it means and what somebody should
> still have of it afterwards, and that is written down before anything is designed.

> **What we do** — We decide the order things happen in, and how long each one is allowed to take. That is
> most of the work, and it is the part that cannot be added at the end.

> **What you do** — You give us the real thing: the place, the people, the work, and time with them. We do
> not build these from a brief and a folder of files.

> **What we argue about** — The person arriving outranks the person paying. They are rarely the same, and
> when the two want different things we argue for whoever will be standing there. We say so at the start.

> Which is why we take very few projects a year. Attention does not divide.
