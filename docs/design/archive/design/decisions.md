# Design decisions

*Running log. Opened 4 August 2026, at the start of the design phase.*
*Foundation is locked: `01-vision.md`, `02-positioning.md`, `03-design-principles.md`, `04-visual-language.md`, `05-storyboard.md`.*

---

Every entry carries the line it is traced to. An entry that cannot name one is not a decision, it is a preference, and it is deleted rather than argued about.

Entries are dated and never rewritten. When one turns out to be wrong it is superseded by a later entry that says so.

**How the work these entries describe is finished is not recorded here.** That is
`docs/development/01-validation.md`, which applies to every implementation without being asked for.
This log holds *what* was decided and why; that document holds the standard the decision had to meet
before it counted as done.

## 01 — The ground is a location, not a theme

Neither dark nor light is the studio's resting state. `04-visual-language.md` §2 says dark is *before* and *inside*, and light is *present* and *plain*. That is a description of two places, so we treat it as one.

**The work is entered by going dark. The studio speaks in plain light.**

The evening is dark for its entire length, because we are inside it. The studio's first words arrive with the change to plain light. Everything practical — how the work happens, writing to us, anything the studio says in its own voice — lives in plain light permanently.

Consequences:
- Crossing between the two is crossing a threshold, and it stays the slowest thing that ever happens (§2).
- No inversion for effect. A ground is where somebody is standing, not a mood applied to a moment.
- Nothing offers a choice of ground. A preference control would make the two states themes, which is exactly what §2 refuses.
- Entering any project, ever, means going indoors. That holds for the second piece and the twentieth.

**Resolves** `open-decisions.md` §10, and resolves it as *both, with a rule* rather than by picking one.

## 02 — Words live in the shadow

Type never crosses the subject of a frame. It occupies the dark part of a composition, and nowhere else.

**Traced to:** `04-visual-language.md` §5 — *a frame is chosen, not inherited.* A word laid across a photograph changes a composition somebody made on purpose. Also §4 — type's expression comes from the space around it — and `03-design-principles.md` §5, we are not the subject.

The consequence is a capture requirement, not a matter of placement:

**Every frame in the evening is composed with shadow to spare.** The photographer is leaving room for the studio's voice at the moment of shooting, in camera, on the night. A frame that is beautiful edge to edge and has no darkness to give is a frame we cannot speak over — and it is cut, however good it is.

This is why the piece can hold language at all without breaking a single image.

## 03 — The studio speaks. On this piece it never reads.

`04-visual-language.md` §4 says type does two jobs, to speak and to disappear, and that recognising them as two jobs is the decision. The homepage needs only the first.

The studio's voice appears five times in two minutes and never exceeds one sentence. There is no long-form reading anywhere in the piece.

Consequences:
- The speaking face is chosen now, and chosen against the five real specimens rather than in the abstract: eleven words in shadow at the size a person speaks · eight words of somebody's actual speech · four words · three statements in plain light · one question asked of the visitor.
- The quiet face is not chosen now. It waits for real long-form material — writing about the work, a second project — because choosing it against invented paragraphs is how a face gets picked for how it looks in a specimen.

**Splits** `open-decisions.md` §7 into two decisions with different deadlines. Only the first is ours this month.

## 04 — One way out, from the first second, never announced

The evening must be leavable from any moment: `03-design-principles.md` §2 gives the pace to the visitor, and `05-storyboard.md` Part One §1 requires a fast route past the atmosphere for anyone who was sent here. But `05` Beat 1 forbids anything competing for the first look.

So the way out exists from second zero. It is singular, it sits in shadow under decision 02, and it never moves, never follows anyone down, never grows. It is the only persistent thing in the piece.

**Unresolved, and first on the design list:** its exact form. `04-visual-language.md` §10 demands it be *understood before it is used* while also being *quiet when not wanted*, and those two pull against each other harder here than anywhere else in the work. Solving that tension is the first thing we design, not the last.

## 05 — The name arrives one hundred seconds in

Nothing greets anyone. `05-storyboard.md` Beat 1 forbids it, and the treatment defers the studio's first word about itself until the ground changes.

That places the first appearance of *Chapter One Studio* after an empty room, in plain light, following an admission that nobody commissioned the thing just watched.

Consequence, and it is a hard test: **a drawn mark has to survive arriving there.** Most cannot. A mark introduces a brand; at that moment we are not introducing a brand, we are conceding something. The name is therefore set in type unless a mark can be shown to work in that specific position, in that light, after that silence.

**Reframes** `open-decisions.md` §9. The question is no longer *wordmark or type* in the abstract. It is whether any mark can hold at 1:42.

## 06 — The homepage is a room, not a story

**Supersedes the premise of the treatment,** which staked the homepage's identity on one occasion told end to end.

`04-visual-language.md` §1 is explicit: the system is a room whose job is to hold material it did not create, and the studio layer exists to be underneath. A homepage that *is* a single story is a picture. When the story is replaced, the identity goes with it — which is the failure the two-layer boundary was written to prevent.

So the homepage is built as an occasion-shaped architecture that any singular moment can pass through. Arrival, fullness, the exhale, emptiness, the turn. Which occasion fills it belongs to the project layer and changes entirely (§1's table).

This makes the homepage a stronger proof rather than a weaker one. A room that holds someone else's material without imposing on it is the thing the studio sells. Demonstrating that is worth more than demonstrating one good story.

**What this does not change:** decisions 01 through 05 all hold unaltered. None of them depended on the occasion, which is the first evidence that the studio layer was separated correctly.

**The hazard it introduces,** and the central design problem from here: specificity was doing the emotional work. Swappable architecture filled with unspecific material is the moody-atmosphere failure — indistinguishable from the studios `02-positioning.md` §6 defines us against. Therefore the architecture must be built so that **only specific material fits it.** A void requiring a sentence somebody actually said cannot be filled with a line somebody wrote. Every void is shaped to reject the generic.

## 07 — Material is specified, never stood in for

`05-storyboard.md` §12 forbids judging the design with placeholders. Decision 06 requires designing before material exists. Both hold, under one rule:

**Every place material will go is designed as a requirement, not as a filled space.** Shape, the shadow it must contain, what must be true of the moment it depicts, what it must not show. The requirement is the deliverable and it is what gets reviewed.

Nothing is signed off as composed until real material has met the requirement. A moment that looks resolved with a stand-in image in it has not been designed; it has been arranged.

## 08 — The public voice is "we"; the founder's story is "I"

Beliefs, methods and experiences are the studio's — *we*. The founding and personal decisions are the founder's — *I*. The studio is the product; the founder is responsible for it.

**Traced to** `01-vision.md`, which is written in the collective throughout and closes on *we made it ours*, and `02-positioning.md` §1, where the sentence is used verbatim in that voice.

Consequence: the admission at the turn is *we*. Any first-person line appears only where the subject is genuinely biographical, and never in the same breath as a claim about the work.

## 09 — English is the design language

One voice, one typographic system, one experience to design against. Portuguese is evaluated after Version 1 and is not designed for now. Client communication happens in whatever language the client prefers — that is a business practice, not a design constraint, and it does not enter the work.

Design consequence, and it is smaller than it appears: Portuguese needs only standard Latin diacritics, so almost any face that satisfies English satisfies it too. Choosing English-first forecloses very little, which means this decision costs us nothing later if the answer changes.

## 10 — The way out is one word, and it is the same door as the turn

Resolves the tension left open in decision 04.

A single word, **Studio**, in shadow, present from the first frame, fixed for the entire length of the piece. Never moving, never following, never growing, never joined by a second word.

Why that word rather than an escape label: *skip* would be understood instantly and would also inform the visitor that what they are watching is an obstacle. **Studio** names the destination instead of the exit, which means it does two jobs at once. For anyone who was sent here it is precisely the thing they came for. For the cold visitor it is the first quiet evidence that somebody made this on purpose — which is exactly what `05-storyboard.md` §3 says Arrival requires in order to move on.

**Traced to:** `04-visual-language.md` §10 — chrome earns permanence by being needed continuously, and `05-storyboard.md` Part One §1 requires the route past the atmosphere be reachable at any point, so it is needed continuously and the permanence is earned by the letter of the rule. Also §10's *understood before it is used*: a word in the visitor's own language needs no learning, where a symbol would.

**And it is not a separate mechanism.** Using it early performs the same threshold crossing as the turn: the room goes, plain light arrives. There is exactly one transition in the entire system, whether it is reached at one hundred seconds or at nine. Leaving early is not an escape hatch added to the work — it is the same door, used sooner. The way back in is that door reversed.

**Capture requirement this creates:** one region of darkness, in the same place, in every frame of the piece. The word lives there and never moves, so the dark has to be there every time. That is now the photographer's second standing constraint alongside decision 02.

# Version 1 — the opening sequence

*Superseding note: the brief for V1 specifies its own opening — a timestamp, a reveal, the
identity, a line, then the interface. Decisions 05 and 10 above described a different
architecture (the name at one hundred seconds, the word `Studio` present from the first
frame) and do not govern V1. They are kept because the reasoning still holds for the longer
piece; they are not what was built.*

## 11 — The footage was measured, not assumed

`public/media/hero/video/header.mp4` — 2560 × 1440, exactly 16:9, 9.45s, H.264.

Measured frame by frame in the browser rather than guessed at:

- **0.00–2.50s** — a wide aerial landscape, layered ridges under heavy overcast, cool and
  desaturated. Gentle motion. Mean luminance 85. Nobody in it.
- **2.50–2.95s** — a cross-dissolve, not a cut. Luminance falls 85 → 61.7.
- **2.95–9.45s** — a figure in silhouette on a ridge, backlit by the sun, warm. Nearly
  static, frame-to-frame difference around 0.5. The lower half of the frame is absolute
  black, measuring 0.7 and 0.0 in the bottom third and bottom strip.

Every timing in the sequence is derived from those numbers. Nothing is a round figure
chosen because it sounded patient.

The footage's own arc is an empty landscape resolving into a person in the light, which is
`04-visual-language.md` §2 without anyone having to arrange it.

## 12 — The identity sits in the shadow the footage already contains

The brief says the identity is centred. Dead centre of both shots is the subject — a lit
valley, then the sun and the figure standing in it. A large title there lands on the
protagonist.

So *centred* is honoured on the horizontal axis and the identity sits at 71% of the frame
height, inside the black the footage already provides.

**Traced to** the brief's own higher instruction — the photography remains the protagonist —
and to `04-visual-language.md` §5, a frame is chosen, not inherited. Decision 02 said words
live in the shadow; this footage supplies the shadow, so nothing had to be dimmed to make
room for language. No scrim, no gradient, no overlay anywhere in the sequence.

## 13 — The frame is a real box of the footage's ratio

The opening is not a full-bleed background. A box of 16:9 is fitted inside the viewport and
the type is positioned relative to *it*, so the words are composed into the photograph
rather than laid over the screen, and the image is never reshaped to fit.
`04-visual-language.md` §5.

Whatever darkness is left around the frame is not a defect. It is the room the frame hangs
in, and on a wide display it is where the interface lives.

**The exception, taken once:** in portrait a 16:9 strip in the middle of a tall screen is a
leftover rather than a composition, so the footage is cropped there. The brief permits a
crop "if absolutely necessary" and this is the only place it is taken. It is a stopgap:
`05-storyboard.md` §9 is owed a separately composed vertical frame and has not been given
one.

## 14 — The identity arrives on the dissolve

First attempt had the identity land at 3.8s and the footage roll at 4.6s, which put the
title over textured hillside for two and a half seconds before the dissolve rescued it. It
was legible and it was fighting the image.

Now the identity begins arriving at 5.3s and the footage dissolves at 6.1s, so the name is
still faint while the ground is busy and reaches full strength as the ground goes black.
The room's light falls as the name comes up.

Measured on the running page: timestamp 386ms · light 1889ms · footage rolls 3600ms ·
identity 5282ms · line 7076ms at footage 3.49s · interface 8482ms.

## 15 — The line is gated, and the footage is the guard

The line is admitted only when the identity has settled *and* the footage is past its
dissolve. Under normal playback the gate is what admits it; the footage check exists so
that a late or stalled start can never put the line on the first shot.

Honest note: the first version claimed the footage triggered the line. It did not — the
gate bound first. The comment was wrong before the code was.

## 16 — Opacity, one curve, and nothing else

No blur, no split text, no travel, no scale, no second curve. Every arrival in the sequence
is a change in light, which is the only change the sequence is about.
`04-visual-language.md` §7.

The footage does not loop. It ends on a figure that is already almost still, and returning
to a cold empty landscape would undo the arc. It plays once and holds.
`04-visual-language.md` §6 — if a still does the same work, it is the still.

## 17 — Leaving early lands where the composition is

Any intent — wheel, key, pointer, touch — collapses the sequence at 320ms rather than
removing it. It also carries the footage past the dissolve, because the end state was
composed against the second shot's black and arriving there early must not mean arriving
somewhere the type was never designed to sit.

**Traced to** `03-design-principles.md` §2, and to the condition that everyone gets a
designed version rather than the same thing with parts taken out.

The same condition drove the no-JavaScript case: without scripting the composed end state
is what arrives, because every element otherwise starts at zero opacity and a visitor with
scripting off would have been shown an entirely black page. `05-storyboard.md` §10.

## 18 — The footage changed to `hero_demo2.mov`, and every timing moved with it

Supersedes the measurements in entry 11. `header.mp4` is no longer used.

`hero_demo2.mov` — 2880 × 1440, **2:1**, 11.888s, H.264 video and AAC audio in a QuickTime
container, encoded with libavcodec.

Re-measured, because every number in the sequence derives from the file rather than from
taste:

- **0.00–3.80s** — the wide aerial landscape. Mean luminance 76, frame difference ~1.8.
- **4.00–4.40s** — a dissolve, and this time it goes *up*: luminance 76 → 88.6.
- **4.40–11.89s** — a closer, warmer framing of the figure against the sun. Luminance holds
  at ~88 with gentle motion throughout. No third shot.

What that forced:

- **The frame ratio.** 2:1, not 16:9. Left unchanged, the frame box would have cropped the
  footage — the exact thing the box exists to prevent. On a wide display the bars fall from
  180px to 73px, so the frame now nearly fills the viewport.
- **The guard.** `SECOND_SHOT_AT` 2.95 → 4.4.
- **The schedule.** The dissolve is 1.5s later in this file, which bought back the frozen
  first frame: the footage now rolls at 2400ms instead of 3600ms, so the freeze window is
  **512ms** rather than 1.71s and the image is alive as light enters it. The identity moves
  to 5400ms so it is still faint over the first shot and reaches full strength as the frame
  changes. Measured: timestamp 370 · light 1873 · footage rolls 2385 · identity 5362 at
  footage 2.99s · line 7065 at footage 4.69s · interface 8465.
- **The composition held.** Shot two is brighter than shot one, inverting the old file, so
  the assumption that the lower frame goes black had to be rechecked. It survives: by 10%
  bands the second shot reads 118, 130, 136, 143, 137, 114, 70, 25, 8, 2 from top to bottom.
  Dead centre is 143 — the brightest part of the frame, and still no place for a title. The
  identity stays at 71%, where the column through the figure falls from 46 to 17, giving
  white type roughly 13:1. No scrim was needed here either.

**The container is a delivery problem, not a design one.** Chrome reports
`canPlayType('video/quicktime')` as empty — no support — and plays the file anyway when
handed the bytes, which is why it is referenced by `src` with no `type` hint: declaring
`video/quicktime` on a `<source>` would make Chrome discard it unplayed. Safari plays MOV;
Firefox generally will not. The stream is already H.264, so remuxing to MP4 with the index
moved to the front is a container operation with no re-encode and no quality loss, and it
fixes the container risk and the trailing-`moov` round trip together. Until then, a Firefox
visitor gets the sequence with no photography in it.

## 19 — Refinement pass: the loop, and where the name arrives

Footage is now `hero_demo3.mov` — 2880 × 1440, still 2:1, 14.652s, and **three shots**:
landscape to 3.4s, a dissolve to 3.6s, the figure to 11.2s, a second dissolve to 11.4s,
then the landscape again.

**The footage is a palindrome.** Its shot boundaries are symmetrical about the middle
(14.652 − 3.5 = 11.15) and frames near the end have near-identical partners near the start.
Comparing every frame in 13.0–14.6s against every frame in 0–1.2s, the best pair is
**14.30s → 0.30s at a difference of 0.92**, against a natural adjacent-frame difference of
1.84 in the same footage. It was cut to loop.

So the loop is trimmed to that measured pair rather than wrapped at the file's ends, and it
is checked every animation frame rather than on `timeupdate` — which fires about four times
a second and would overshoot the seam by up to a quarter of a second, landing the restart
somewhere that was never measured.

Verified on the running page, capturing the post-seek frame only after it had actually
decoded: the seam runs 14.295 → 0.314 with a difference of **0.72**, below the 90th
percentile of normal frame-to-frame change (0.74), and playback advanced 0.402s in the
following 400ms — no stall. **One decoder, one seek, no crossfade.** Nothing is paid for at
runtime, which is why this was chosen over the usual two-element crossfade.

**The name now arrives inside the light rather than after it.** The reveal was lengthened to
3200ms so it is still coming up when the identity begins, and the identity moved from 5400ms
to 4100ms.

Measured, and reproducible to within 3ms across runs: timestamp 367 · light 1568 · timestamp
leaves 2066 · footage rolls 2080 · **identity 4065** · line 5867 at footage 3.80s · interface
7668.

Two consequences worth recording:

- **A full second of the photograph alone.** The timestamp has gone by 3066ms and the
  identity does not begin until 4065ms, so the image is experienced before the name.
- **The identity settles at 5666ms; the footage finishes its dissolve at 5680ms.** Fourteen
  milliseconds apart. The name reaches full strength at the exact instant the frame changes,
  which is the best moment in the sequence and is the footage's doing, not ours.

**The line is the second level of the identity, not a caption.** 19px against the title's
96px — a 1:5 ratio — brightness from 0.62 to 0.78, tracking from 0.06em to 0.045em. Grouping
does the hierarchy: 44px from the title, 62px to the interface, so the pair binds and the
interface reads as a different kind of thing.

## 20 — Refinement pass: full bleed, and the identity at the centre

Two entries above are **superseded by direction**, not by argument, and the record should say
so plainly.

**Decision 13 is withdrawn.** The frame is no longer a box of the footage's own ratio; the
footage now fills the viewport at every size and crops. That is a deliberate override of
`04-visual-language.md` §5 — *an image is never reshaped to fit the space it lands in* — and
it is the client's call, made knowing the trade. Recorded here rather than quietly dropped,
because a traceability log that only holds the decisions that survived is worthless.

Two things the sweep caught that the eye would not have:

- **`width: 100%` left an eleven pixel black strip down the right edge on every screen** —
  the scrollbar's gutter. The opening is now `100vw` and bleeds under the scrollbar, with
  `overflow-x: hidden` on the root to stop the sideways scroll that creates.
- **`100svh` is the viewport with mobile toolbars showing.** The moment they retract the
  screen grows and the hero stops reaching the bottom. Now `100dvh`, with `100vh` beneath it
  for anything that does not understand it.

Measured across 16:9, 21:9, two laptops, tablet in both orientations, two phones, square and
5:12: every one reports zero gap on both axes and no letterbox. Confirmed visually as well,
by painting each test viewport magenta so any uncovered pixel would be obvious. None was.

**Decision 12 is partly withdrawn.** The identity is now at the optical centre — the place
the timestamp occupied — rather than in the shadow at 71%. The cost is measurable and worth
writing down: behind the title the crop reads mean luminance 145, which gives white type
**3.15:1**, just past the 3:1 that large text requires, and **1.43:1 against the brightest
part of the sun's bleed**. It holds up because the middle of the word crosses the figure's
dark silhouette, but the thin strokes lose definition to the left of centre. The second level
is safer at 5.17:1.

If that ever needs recovering without moving the title, the lever is stroke weight rather
than a scrim — thin type on bright ground is what fails here, not the placement.

**The pause before the identity is gone.** It now begins at ~3050ms, the instant the
timestamp has finished leaving, and its whole fade sits inside the light's arrival, so the
name comes up with the first landscape instead of after it. A consequence: the identity now
settles at ~4650ms, before the footage's dissolve rather than exactly on it, so the
coincidence recorded in entry 19 is spent. What replaced it is better anyway — the *line* is
now admitted by the footage, arriving at exactly 3.60s of its own time, so the second level
lands with the second shot as the original brief asked.

**Entry 19's custom loop is removed, and it should be.** The file is cut to loop and native
`loop` proves it: the seam runs 14.652 → 0.015 at a difference of **0.65**, below the 90th
percentile of ordinary playback (0.80) and marginally cleaner than the 0.72 my trimmed seam
achieved. The measurement was worth keeping; the machinery was not.

## 56 — The studio goes dark to listen, and comes back with your answer printed on its page

*20 August 2026. Supersedes §55's room, field and resolution; keeps its metaphor, its copy and its runway
entirely. Chapters I to III, About, the questions and Contact are untouched — no file outside the method's own
four was edited except the barrel that exports the motion module. The film was replayed from the first frame.*

§55 built the method as a perspective field on paper: four questions, twelve considerations at their own
distances, and a convergence into one line. **The mechanism was right and the physics were not.** Measured at
1440 × 900 before this pass:

| | §55 | §56 |
|---|---|---|
| the fullest frame's ink | **1.46 %** of the frame in bounding boxes, ~0.2 % in actual ink | ~6 % |
| the whole depth range, drawn | **24.4px → 18.0px** — six pixels | **55.8px → 19.2px** |
| words overlapping any other | none, by construction | three, deliberately |
| frames where nothing changes on scroll | every gap between two cues | none |
| the ground | paper, identical to the page above and below | the publication's own ink |
| the resolution | 50px, then the frame drained for two thirds of a screen | 83.5px, printed, handed to the questions |

A `rotateY` of 10° on a 110px word at that distance made its near edge **1.05 %** larger than its far edge.
That is below the threshold of perception. The section was paying for a perspective it never showed anybody,
and the eye read the six-pixel scale range not as distance but as inconsistent type. Everything below follows
from those two numbers.

### The ground turns over, and it is not a second ground

The paper darkens to `#181714` as the section is approached, the whole piece is spoken in `#f4f1e9` on it, and
then the paper comes back and the answer is printed on it.

**§54 says the film has already spent both of the grounds it gets — black for the evening, paper for the
studio — and this does not spend a third.** `#181714` is `--ink`'s own colour at full strength, and `#f4f1e9`
is `--paper` exactly. The publication has two materials and this is those two materials with their roles
exchanged: the same page, seen from the other side. It is deliberately *not* the film's black, which would say
the film had resumed.

Three things keep it from reading as a fourth chapter, and all three are absences: no numeral, no marker, and
no change to the masthead — which is `position: fixed` and paper, so **the page's own head margin stays paper
for the whole of the section** while the field beneath it inverts. That strip is the single clearest statement
that this is still the publication. Nothing in the navigation was touched.

Every colour in the section is a `color-mix` on one property, `--mroom`, and that is what makes the rest
correct for nothing: at rest `--mroom` is 0, and 0 *is* the publication. The version with no scripting needed
no new rule for its ink.

### The entrance is a band, not a beat

The darkening happens on the approach, over `methodArrival.begins` — **0.86 viewport heights above the lock**,
which starts it while About is still on the screen and its own photograph, a dark room at night with one lamp
in it, is still in the frame. The room in the picture becomes the room the visitor is in. It settles 0.05
short of the lock, so the frame is never seen to arrive and then go dark; it is dark when it arrives.

**It is stated in viewport heights and not in beats, and that is load-bearing rather than fussy.** The approach
is a layout distance — a viewport, less the head margin, plus a band — so it is the same length however the
runway is priced. Beats are not: `--method-pin` is half as long again for a thumb, so the identical stretch of
scroll is 2.2 beats on a wheel and 1.4 on a thumb, and a range authored in beats to fit one runs off the end
of the other. It is the first value in the project that the driver computes outside a track, and the comment
there says why.

**The ramp needed a band of its own, and finding out why cost a pass.** Painted on the held frame, the
gradient from paper to room sat *inside* the viewport that gets held — so the top 180px of the room was still
fading to paper for the entire section. It is now `.method`'s own `padding-top`, above the frame, and the
section's height carries it so the content box is still exactly `100dvh + --method-pin` and the runway is
unchanged. `scroll-stage.tsx` adds the same padding when it measures the lock, read from the rendered box
rather than from a copy.

### The depth was rebuilt four ways, and only one of them is scale

**Scale.** 55.8px at the front and 19.2 at the back, 2.9:1, against §55's 1.35:1. The near words are display
type now; the far ones are the size the whole field used to be.

**Occlusion.** Three of the twelve cross behind another — `Light` behind `Place`, `Names` behind `People`,
`Detail` behind `Photographs` — each a word at the back tucking under the edge of one at the front, at between
a half and a third of its size. Occlusion is the strongest depth cue there is and the only one that cannot be
mistaken for a difference in type size. The browser sorts them for nothing, because they are real siblings at
real depths in one `preserve-3d` context.

But sorting is only half of it: **type has no ground of its own**, so a far word passing behind a near one
simply interleaves its glyphs and reads as a collision. The first build of this looked like a rendering bug.
Each consideration now carries a soft halo of the room's own colour, in `em` so it scales with the word — the
room seen between the letters, which is what would be there. It exists only while `--mroom` does, so there is
no shadow on paper and none in the no-script version.

**The camera.** `--mdrift` runs the length of the accumulation and every word takes `1 - --wd` of it, so the
near words travel about seventeen times as far as the far ones and the space separates under movement. The
nearest word moves roughly a pixel for every ten the page does. This is the fix for the complaint that had
nothing to do with depth: §55's field was *still* between arrivals, and a held frame that does not change when
you scroll is the exact feeling of a prototype.

It is the one range in the project resolved with **no curve** — `ramp` in `scroll.ts`, straight. Every other
range is a beat, something that arrives, so it eases. A camera that eased in at the top of the runway and out
at the bottom would read as an animation of the space rather than as movement through it, and the easing would
be most visible exactly where a visitor is most likely to stop.

The questions and the answer do not drift. They stand at the focus, the focus is the vanishing point, and
nothing at a vanishing point moves when you walk toward it — which also leaves one fixed thing in the frame
for everything else to be visibly moving against.

**Ink.** Paper on this ink is a 15.9:1 ground, so the falloff can be much steeper than it could on paper and
still measure: 0.96 → 0.52 puts the farthest word at **5.5:1 at 19.2px**, against §55's 4.86:1 at 18.6 on
paper. Depth got stronger and legibility got better at the same time, which is the only version of that trade
worth taking.

### There was a lamp, and it is gone

The obvious idea — a soft radial lift at the focus, continuing About's single light source — was built and
removed. On this ground it **contoured**: 0.055 of paper over this ink is a delta of twelve values, and twelve
values spread across seven hundred pixels puts a visible banding ring every sixty. Eight bits is eight bits.
The only two fixes are a dither texture, which is an image this section does not want, or a steeper gradient,
which is a spotlight.

The room is flat ink instead, and that is the better answer rather than the fallback: `03-design-principles.md`
§3 says silence is material, and an even field of the studio's own ink is material where a lit set is
scenography. All four depth cues above are measured; none of them is atmosphere.

### The resolution happens twice, and the sequencing is not a preference

`Your experience` comes forward out of the point everything collapsed into, stands alone in the dark room for
`resolve.holds` — a beat §55 did not have, so the composition it resolved into was never once seen standing —
and then the frame **clears**, the paper **returns**, and the answer is **printed**: the same words, the same
size, the same place on the axis, in the publication's own ink.

**It is three stages and not a cross-fade because of a fact about light.** Light type becoming dark type while
the ground behind it becomes light passes through a frame where both have the same luminance: measured at
about **1.1:1**, which is invisible, on the one line the whole section exists to deliver. So the type leaves an
intact room, the empty room lightens, and the answer arrives on a page. `actStory.printing.clears` is the same
decision made about Chapter III's own type for the same reason, and `timeline.ts` now asserts the two never
overlap so a ripple edit cannot quietly reintroduce it.

The two authored lines exist **only on paper**. In §55 they arrived in the dark under the answer and the
section ended, which made its last frame the previous frame with a caption added. After the printing, the
composition the visitor leaves on is fuller than any frame in the room was.

And the handoff is a handoff: the frame releases onto paper that is already paper, with the resolution standing
in it, and the questions' rule and label climb into the same frame underneath it. The two thirds of a screen
of dead white §55 ended on is gone — not by shortening anything, but because there is nothing left to drain.

The method was four labelled moves and a consequence — *How we start · What we do · What you do · What we

delivered as written, every line of it traceable, and it was **prose about a method**. It told the visitor

**This section now performs it.** The studio's questions arrive one at a time; each brings the considerations
it hears in the answer; twelve of them accumulate at their own distances in a space; and then every one of

The copy is in `copy-drafts.md`. If those practical answers are wanted in words again they belong as rows in

### The metaphor, and the one decision it turns on

```
four questions   →   twelve considerations   →   one point   →   Your experience
```

The decision that makes it hold together is **where that point is**: the questions stand on the page's own
axis, the considerations are placed as offsets *from that same point*, they converge back into it, and the
answer emerges from it. One place. The section is not a sequence of arrivals that happens to end on a line —
it is a place being filled and then resolved, and the last thing to stand there is what everything else
became.

That axis is `--mark-x`, which is the vertical the mark in the head margin, the studio's type over the work,
the line under the plate and every label in the publication already share. The one moment this section
resolves belongs on it, and centring it would have made it ceremonial (`04-visual-language.md` §5).

### The space is real, and it is CSS

`perspective: 1400px` on the frame with `perspective-origin` at the focus, and every consideration placed with
one `translate3d` and a `rotateY`. Four authored numbers per word and nothing else:

```
--wx, --wy   where it stands, as an offset from the focus, in vw and vh
--wd         how far away it is, 0 at the front and 1 at the back
--wr         how far it is turned — every word is angled toward the focus
--wo         which of the twelve arrivals is its own
```

`--wd` drives three things at once: the distance in z, the type size and the ink. So **a word cannot be far
away and loud**, and depth is one decision rather than three that have to be kept in agreement. The
projection then does the rest: it scales a far word to 73% and pulls its position toward the origin in
proportion to its distance, so the space recedes toward the point it will resolve into. That compression is
why the two farthest words are authored at 100vw and 108vw and land at about 70% of it.

No WebGL, no library, no canvas, and **no lines drawn between anything**. A network of connections is a
diagram, and a diagram explains a method instead of performing one. The only vectors in the section are the
twelve the words travel along when they converge, and those are motion rather than marks.

### The convergence is one number

`--mgather` runs 0 → 1 and `--mspread` is `1 - mgather`, which multiplies every offset in the field. Twelve
elements collapse toward one point on twelve different vectors, coming forward as they go, and **not one of
them is animated individually**. It is the same construction `--aperture` uses to add the two halves of
Chapter III's reveal: one property, arithmetic in the stylesheet, no second opinion anywhere.

Two mechanical traps were found and are worth recording, because both are silent:

**An opacity on a wrapper flattens a 3D scene.** The obvious way to fade the field out is a fade on the
element holding it; `opacity` (like `filter` and `overflow`) forces a flattening that discards `preserve-3d`,
and the whole perspective goes with it. So each word multiplies its own arrival by `--mspread` instead, and
every layer between the frame and the words carries `preserve-3d` and nothing that would flatten it.

**A `ch` limit is measured in the element's own font size.** `.mresolve` was given `width: min(28ch, …)` and
came out 224px wide, because the wrapper is set at 16px while the answer inside it is 50px — so the answer
wrapped and both authored lines wrapped with it. It is `max-content` now, with the answer set `nowrap`.

### The third runway, and why it earns one

`methodPin` / `METHOD_BEATS` / `methodTrack`, alongside the shot's and the act's. Accumulation needs a frame
that stays still while it fills; convergence needs the filled frame to still be there when it collapses.
Neither is possible in flow.

**It is deliberately the shortest and cheapest held frame on the site**, and the numbers are the argument:

| | held for | a beat costs |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter I → II, the shot | 392vh | 54.7vh |
| Chapter III, the act | 176vh | 55.0vh |
| The method | 160vh | 42.1vh |

A beat of the act weighs what a beat of the shot weighs, because it is the third act of the same film. A beat
of the method weighs a fifth less, because it is **not a chapter** and must not feel like one in the hand. The
whole section is thirteen notches of a wheel from the first word to the last, and every beat in it is hit at
least once at that pace: measured, one notch is 0.267 beats, and each question is at full strength on one.

Its beats are numbered from the moment its frame is stuck under the head margin. `.method` is measured rather
than the stage, because a stuck element's rect reports the sticky offset rather than where it came from — a
resize while the section is stuck would otherwise read its own offset back as its position.

**A question's dwell is not a guess.** Each is present for 0.5 beats, which at this price is 21vh — the same
budget Chapter II gives each of its four occasions (26vh) for the same kind of thing, one line read once. The
0.22 hold was *bought* rather than added: `opening.hold` gave up 0.04 and `gathered.holds` 0.02, so the dwell
went up a tenth and the runway did not move.

### The properties are written on the section, not on the root

Twenty of them, and this is the first place in the project that does not write to `:root`. Setting a custom
property on the root invalidates style for the entire document; the method's are read by nothing outside its
own frame, so a frame of its choreography would otherwise re-resolve the film, the publication and a
cross-origin iframe along with itself. The shot and the act still write to the root, because their properties
are genuinely read in two places.

Measured after: median frame 6.9ms and worst 7.8ms through a full pass of the section on a 144Hz display —
no dropped frames. Everything animated is `opacity` and `transform`, there is no filter, no shadow and no
blur, and nothing carries `will-change`: twenty small text nodes do not need a promoted layer each, and
`04-visual-language.md`'s own budget is one thing moving at a time, not twenty compositor layers.

### Four questions, and what the studio hears

Every question is one the studio actually asks, and each is traceable: *what is the moment* is
`02-positioning.md` §3's first test; *who will be standing there* is §5's uncomfortable condition in its own
words — the person arriving, not the person paying; *what should it feel like* is §3's second test; *what
should remain* is `01-vision.md`'s *something that outlives the day*.

Every consideration is a **concrete noun** (§12): Time · Place · Light · People · Names · Distance · Silence ·
Pace · Colour · Detail · Photographs · Memory. Not categories, not services, not values — the things an
occasion is actually made of. Three of them are the studio's own instruments, which is the quiet admission
that the answer to *what should it feel like* is a set of decisions rather than an adjective.

The last group is ordered against the pattern on purpose: `Detail` is the farthest and `Memory` the nearest,
because `Memory` is the last word to arrive before everything converges and the last thing in the frame

### Legibility was the correction, twice

The first version put the farthest word at 13px and **2.5:1** against the paper, which is atmosphere bought
with reading — `03-design-principles.md` names that trade and forbids it. This near-black on this paper loses
contrast fast: an alpha of 0.41 measures 2.5:1 where 0.65 measures 5.2. So the depth was rebalanced to carry
itself in scale, position and turn, and only lean on ink: the range is now 0.9 → 0.6, the projection takes 27%
instead of 31%, and the type barely falls at all. Measured across all twelve at 1920: **worst 4.86:1 at
18.6px**, best 11.1:1 at 25.3px. The same check on a phone in landscape produced the same correction a second
time, at a smaller scale.

### Mobile is a different composition, not a smaller one

**And it turns at 700 rather than at 900, where the rest of the publication turns.** That is not an
inconsistency — a rail gives up when its second column falls under fifty characters, and a field gives up
when there is no width left to place anything in. Measured at 768, the wide field spans 234 → 683 of the
frame and reads as a space; the column reached 300 and left the right half empty. So a tablet keeps the space
and a phone gets the column.

The narrow composition is a **column with a gap in it**: four considerations above the question's own line and

axis (0.72 and 0.55) rather than a third set of coordinates. At 844 × 390 the frame is 291 tall and the last
word would otherwise be clipped; every relationship in the layout survives being drawn smaller.

### Without scripting

There is no frame and no choreography, so the section is told down the page instead: the invitation, then each

**⟲ revised. `afterTheFilm.about` is the second, and it is the one composed arrival in the publication.**
The first pass left About entirely static — correct by the letter of §7 (*if nothing has changed in meaning,
nothing moves*) and wrong in the room: the section where the studio shows you where the work is made was the
one place on the page that did not behave like anything. What changed in meaning is exactly that, so there is
a small sequence: **the room, then the mark, then the words**, over 2.2 seconds, on a clock, once, triggered
by the same observer everything else down here uses.

The photograph is the only thing that moves — 14px from below (`image.settle`), which is about three per cent
of its height: enough that the arrival has a direction and reads as a picture being set down, little enough
that nobody can name the movement. Above about 24px it is a slide. The label and the three blocks of words are
**opacity alone**, `words.step` apart, because three elements each drifting upward is the generic scroll
reveal this exists instead of. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the settle is zero and the order is unchanged —
not a beat removed, the same arrival in light only, which is the one thing in this piece such a request is
actually about.

It is on a clock rather than on scroll and it is **not a beat**: nothing is pinned, nothing is driven, and
stopping mid-scroll leaves it finished rather than half way. The three word delays are resolved in
`timeline.ts` from one authored `step`, so the cadence is one number.

Everything else that moves down here is the page arriving once — `[data-reveal]`, on the **page** rather than
on the elements in it (§43). The questions are native `<details>`: they open before hydration, without
JavaScript and from the keyboard, and the row's height is the browser's, instantly. No height is measured and
none is interpolated.

### Measured, not estimated

Two things were found by measuring and are worth keeping:

**An authored break gives way before a composition does.** *The world is built for what happens often.* is
41 characters and needs about 760px at the 37px a 1440 column gets in 714 — so `01-vision.md`'s two lines
were arriving as an accidental three. Broken at the verb it became 29 characters, which needs 345px against
the 334 a 390 phone has. The glyph width in this face at weight 300 is 0.476em, so the honest limit at 320
— 264px of measure, 22px of type — is **24 characters**, and every authored line in the publication is
under it. `01-validation.md` names this failure mode; `.page-lead`'s measure was raised from 24ch to 32ch so
the limit doing the work is the one that was argued.

That statement is gone with the essay (**⟲ revised**), but the number it produced is the reason the method's
two lines are 19 and 22 characters, and it is why About's opening sentence is deliberately **not** an authored
composition: it is prose, set as a standfirst, and prose is allowed to wrap. Where it does wrap it is asked to
balance rather than to leave `repeat.` alone on a second line — `text-wrap: balance`, and the only place in the
publication that needs it, because it is the only display type set to an inherited width.

**A long question makes an index look like a paragraph.** *Can you work with the identity and the
photographer we already have?* is 67 characters and took three lines on a phone. The row is now 39
characters and the specifics moved into the answer, where they were always better placed.

## 53 — The work is the frame

*18 August 2026. Supersedes §49 outright, §51 and §52 on most points, and closes the objection §44 opened
and never answered. Chapter III only; Chapters I and II are untouched and were re-measured to prove it.*

Chapter III has been rebuilt four times around one assumption, and the assumption was wrong. §49 put a
device at the centre of the frame; §51 removed the claim in front of it and gave it a press; §52 shortened
the act around it, weighted the composition, and cut the annotation to a name and two lines. Every one of
those was an improvement, and every one of them was arranging furniture around an object that should not
have been there.

**`05-storyboard.md` §8 Beat 3 says, verbatim: *Must not: show a laptop, a phone frame, or a scrolling
screenshot.*** It says the visitor gets *a fragment of the actual experience — a real moment from it,
behaving the way it behaves — rather than a picture of a screen*, and it makes that a construction
requirement decided before the piece is made rather than a crop chosen afterwards. Part One wins over any
implementation. §49 answered the *mockup* objection — no notch, no speaker, no drawn hardware — and never
answered the *frame* objection, for three revisions.

### The principle, in one line

Chapter I's material is footage and its type is composed inside it. Chapter II is the same footage going
dark. **Chapter III is the studio's own project, and its type is composed inside it in exactly the same
way.** Three chapters, one grammar, and the third one finally uses it.

The aperture no longer opens an object. It opens the whole frame, and what is behind it is a live document.

### What the project supplies, and what the studio may assume

One URL. `site.three.work.fragment` — a route the project authored for this, which fills whatever frame it
is given, composes for it from 9:19.5 through 21:9, never scrolls, carries no navigation and no controls,
and is silent. No postMessage, no handshake, no scene protocol, no colour protocol, no shared code, and
nothing in either repository that names the other.

That contract is what makes the studio layer do no arithmetic at all. **The iframe is the frame** —
`inset: 0` of the stage, at the stage's own size. §52 needed a measured viewport, a scrollbar gutter and a
`ResizeObserver` because it was fitting a 390-wide document into a drawn phone; a frame that composes has
nothing to fit, and all three are deleted.

The fragment itself was built first and separately, in the project's own repository, and is recorded there:
the couple's own photograph filling the frame, their names in the site's own serif, `cinematicText` from
its own motion module, `object-cover` at a measured anchor, and one declared quiet edge.

### The light is the whole of the narration

The act has one studio-level variable and it is the one the film is built on. Four states, and the
sequence is nothing but the movement between them:

```
paper       the chapter's own ground, before the aperture
lit         the work, whole, alone, at full strength — nothing said
evening     darkens.depth 0.5 — enough dark to carry white type over a photograph
a trace     deepens.depth 0.88 — the work receded, the studio's line the brightest thing
paper       the light returns as the frame acquires margins and becomes a plate
```

**The atmosphere is gone.** Three radial fields, three closed transform loops on three non-harmonic
periods, a spill layer, `work.atmosphere`, `work.light`, `--aglow`, `--drift-1…3` and
`prefers-reduced-motion: animation none` — all deleted. It failed `04-visual-language.md` §11.4 outright
(*anything that would survive the material being removed*: take the wedding out and the room still glowed
amber) and §1's boundary test (hand-copied hex values from somebody else's stylesheet, living in the
studio's content file, are the studio layer impersonating the project layer). When the work is the frame,
the colour arrives from the material for free, which is what §3 asked for in the first place.

**The warm wash went too, and that is a correction rather than a simplification.** It was carried over
from a version whose ground was paper, where a single cross-fade to black passes through rgb(127,127,124)
at its midpoint and reads as a screen dimming. A photograph is not neutral: darkening golden-hour
photography with black passes through darker versions of its own colour. Over the work the same wash is a
beige veil that lifts the shadows and mutes everything — `04-visual-language.md` §3 exactly backwards.
Screenshotted, seen, removed. Chapter I already says this in code: one black layer over its footage, two
over the dawn.

**The dark lives inside the aperture**, so it falls on the work and nothing else — not on the chapter's
paper before anything is uncovered, and not on the plate's margins while they are being printed. A page
whose margins darken is not a page being printed.

### The type is composed into the work

Not a column beside it. `04-visual-language.md` §4 says type carries a moment by itself only where there is
no image doing work; here there is one, so the type belongs to it, the way the timestamp belongs to the
hero's photograph.

It stands in the band the fragment declares quiet — its top — on the page's own left axis, so the mark in
the head margin, the annotation over the work and the line printed under the plate all share one vertical.
And the dark is what makes it legible: the studio dims the room to speak about the work rather than laying
a scrim over it. One is narrative; the other is furniture.

Two voices in one frame, and they are meant to be: the material speaks in its own serif at the bottom left
and the studio annotates it in its own sans at the top left. That is what a catalogue plate is.

### One offer, and it is printed rather than offered

§51 had a word inside the device that *entered* the work and a line beside it that *left* with it — the
same verb twice, two hundred pixels apart, and a distinction the visitor could not see in advance. §52 kept
both. There is nothing to enter now: the fragment is material, the experience is elsewhere, and one line
says so.

It arrives **on the paper**, sixty per cent of the way through the printing, set in the page's own ink. It
is the only pressable thing in the chapter and the only link in the act — measured, one `<a>`. *Open the
full experience →*, not *Explore*: there is nothing to explore, the work is already on screen.

`work.embeds`, `work.viewport`, `work.invite`, `work.loading`, `work.away`, `work.atmosphere` and
`work.light` are all deleted. The project object went from eleven fields to five, and the five describe a
shot and a destination rather than how either is presented.

### The film becomes the page, and that is the last movement

§51 ended the act on a second offer over near-black and let the chapter's closing arrive on paper beneath
it — so the ground changed at a section boundary, and `04-visual-language.md` §2 makes a change of ground an
*event*. An event in a scroll seam is an event nobody attends. §52 added a ground beat, which fixed the
seam and still ended on a frame the visitor then scrolled away from.

**`printing` does all of it on one range.** The light comes back, the frame draws in until it has margins,
and what is left standing is a plate on paper with one line under it. The visitor watches the film become
the page.

It is the second element in the whole piece that transforms, and it earns it on exactly the grounds
`04-visual-language.md` §7 gives the first — the word `chapter` travelling into the corner. What changes is
what the thing is *for*: the work stops being the subject of a film and becomes a plate in a publication,
and **the appearance of a margin is that relationship**. §52's crossing object is deleted, so the count is
unchanged: two things move in Chapter One, and no more.

The scale is uniform, so the work keeps the ratio it composed itself for; `transform-origin: top left`
makes one translate and one scale land the plate on the page's own margin, the same construction `.mark`
uses. The distance is `globals.css` (`--plate-scale`, `--plate-x`, `--plate-y`) because where a plate sits
on a page is composition.

### Nested scrolling stops being a category

§52 answered it with `.act-shield`, a transparent layer that stepped out of existence at the pin release.
That was a correct fix to a self-inflicted problem: an interactive document inside a frame that owns the
page's scroll is two contracts in one gesture.

The fragment is material. It has nothing to press and nothing to scroll, so `pointer-events: none`
throughout is the whole answer — a finger on it reaches the page underneath and the page scrolls natively,
with nothing locked and no event swallowed. Measured: `elementFromPoint` at the centre of the work returns
`.act-stage`. The shield, the veil, the scrim, the press, the state machine and `--afree` are all deleted,
and the iframe is `tabIndex={-1}`.

and the act is what was left.

## 52 — The work is the preview, the act is 4.5 beats, and the film ends before the page begins

*18 August 2026. Refines §51 and supersedes it on four points: the annotation's shape, where the way out
sits, how long the act is, and what the act ends on. Chapter III only. Chapters I and II are untouched and
were re-measured to prove it.*

The brief that produced §51 asked for a denser chapter. This one asked for four specific things that §51
did not have: the experience **live behind a veil** rather than fetched on a press, an act of about
**4.5 beats** rather than 5.78 inside 6.4, a composition **weighted to the optical left** rather than two
things centred in a wide field, and an **ending** — one beat of ground transition out of the dark, after
which the site is an ordinary editorial page. It also asked, structurally, that the live document must not
be touchable while the frame owns the page's scroll.

Nothing here is new machinery. Every one of them is either a retiming in `story.ts`, a place in a frame in
`globals.css`, or one transparent layer.

### The experience is the preview, not the reward

§51's glass carried one word on black and nothing behind it. The press fetched the document. That is
backwards for a studio whose claim is that it builds experiences: **the visitor should be looking at one
before they are offered one.**

So `afterTheFilm.experience.mountsWithin` is 1.5 viewports and `timeline.ts` builds the observer's margin
from it — the same construction `studioBlocks.arrivesShortOf` uses, and for the same reason: the number is
the decision and the string is the shape the DOM wants it in. `.act`'s own frame is about six viewports
down the document, so nothing is requested until the visitor is well into Chapter II, and a visitor who
never gets there never pays for it. Measured: by the time the act's frame reaches the top of the viewport,
`data-experience` already reads `veiled` — the document is running, not starting.

The veil is `.act-veil`, a scrim at **0.68** of the glass's own near-black. The wedding experience's cream
falls from 243 to about 87 under it: plainly a real page, plainly not yet the visitor's. Explicitly **not**
a `backdrop-filter` — `globals.css` forbids the blur outright (`04-visual-language.md` §7), and re-filtering
a live cross-origin document every frame would be the most expensive thing on the page on exactly the screen
that can least afford it.

**The whole of the glass is the target.** `.act-ask` is `inset: 0` with the word centred on it, so there is
no control sitting on the preview — pressing the preview is what enters it. It is a `<button>` where the
press lifts the scrim, a real `<a>` with the arrow where it opens a window, and inert where there is
nothing to open, so it is whichever of those it actually is to a keyboard as well as to a cursor. The
iframe is **never** keyed on the press: remounting it would restart the experience, and continuity is the
thing being demonstrated.

One thing the server render forced into the open: with nothing fetched and no press possible, the honest
first paint is a *disabled button*, which is a control that does nothing — the one thing the glass must
never carry. `.act-flat` is the answer: the same offer as a real link, `display: none` for everybody else
so it is out of the tab order, revealed by the `noscript` block.

### The nested-scroll trap, answered with one transparent layer

A scrollable document inside a pinned frame that owns the page's scroll is a trap, and on a phone it is
severe: the object is most of the frame, so a thumb on it moves the wedding site while the homepage stands
still. Scroll locking is not available — the page has to stay native.

`.act-shield` is one transparent layer over the frame, clipped out of existence by `--afree`, which steps
at exactly `ACT_BEATS` — the position the sticky frame reaches the bottom of its box and starts moving with
the document. A touch lands on the shield, does nothing, and the page scrolls natively. Nothing is locked,
no event is swallowed, and the work underneath keeps rendering throughout.

Hit-tested at six positions with the scrim lifted and the work live: `elementFromPoint` over the glass
returns `act-shield` at every beat up to 4.69 and `act-frame` from 4.71. The film is over, and the work is
the visitor's, in the same frame.

### The composition is weighted left; the pair no longer centres

§51 solved `--object-cross` so that object, gap and column ended up centred with equal margins — 525 on
each side at 1920, which reads as two small things marooned rather than as a composition.

It is now solved the other way round. `--object-x` is where the object's left edge lands —
`max(--mark-x, --act-free × --act-weight)` — and `--object-cross` is the distance from its centred rest to
there. One authored number, `--act-weight: 0.34`, and the silence collects on the right, which is the
asymmetry a spread has. The column and the gap grew with it (26rem → 30rem, 4.5rem → 5.5rem) because at
1600 and above the column was the one thing in the frame that could.

Measured, left margin against right: **328 / 636** at 1902, **216 / 418** at 1422, both 1 : 1.94. And
`--screen-h` went from 84 to 88 hundredths of the frame — which does not bind at 1920 × 1080, where
`--screen-fit` wins at 849 against 853, so what it actually buys is the shorter windows in between where
the fraction was binding and the object was small for no reason.

### The annotation is a name and two lines, and the name stays

`01` and `SELECTED EXPERIENCE` are gone, and they were a mistake rather than a matter of taste. An index
numbers a set, and this set has one member — so `01` promised an `02` the act is explicitly composed not to
have (`05-storyboard.md` §8 Beat 3). `SELECTED EXPERIENCE` classified something nobody had asked to have
classified, in the register of a portfolio card, on the one page meant not to be one. `work.kind` went with

What replaced it is the shape every future project gets: **a name and two lines.** And the name is now its

The way out moved with it. §51 offered it last, after everything had been said, on the grounds that nobody
acts into uncertainty — right about the order, wrong about the beat. Last meant the act closed on a second

## 50 — Two clocks in one chapter: scroll owns the room, time owns the object

Chapter III's act is now driven by **two different things at once**. Everything around the device — the
aperture, the light leaving, the colour arriving, the way out — is scroll, exactly as before. Everything
the device *says* is time, on a clock of its own that starts when the visitor has arrived and stopped.

The studio's two sentences have moved off the page and onto the screen.

### Why the manifesto could not stay scroll-driven

§49 put the two sentences in the paper above the object and timed them in beats, which meant the visitor
had to scroll a precise distance to finish reading a sentence. That is not a sentence, it is a control —
and it fails in both directions: scroll a little too fast and the correction to *We don't build websites*
never lands, stop a little early and the frame holds a half-lit line indefinitely.

The distinction that resolves it is not about implementation. **The room changing is something the visitor
does, so it belongs to their hand. A sentence being spoken is something that happens to them.** Once that
is the rule, which unit each thing takes is not a choice any more.

So the act keeps its beats for the room, `afterTheFilm.experience` gains a millisecond schedule for the
object, and the two meet at exactly one place.

### One joint, and it is a position rather than a value

`actStory.speaks` — the only thing either clock knows about the other. It is authored as a *condition of
the room* (`whenDarkIs: 0.85`) and solved backwards on the darkening, like every other join in the act,
because what was decided is that the object may not speak until the light has essentially left. It
resolves to beat 2.40: the ground is 0.765 dark, which is where the screen stops being a dark shape on a
lit page and becomes the lit thing in an unlit room.

It drives nothing. `transitions.ts` states it as a length down the act's runway, `page.tsx` puts a
zero-width sentinel there, and `experience.tsx` watches that sentinel against a root collapsed to a
one-pixel band at the top of the viewport. The whole coupling is one boolean: *is the visitor inside the
stretch where the object is settled.*

A sentinel rather than a scroll listener for the reason `reveal.tsx` gives — the position comes from the
story and the browser reports the crossing for free — and it spans the stretch rather than marking a line,
because a flick carries two thousand pixels and a zero-height mark is only intersecting for the frames it
is actually crossing.

`timeline.ts` asserts the one thing that is not true by construction: **`speaks` must land after the
aperture has finished.** The two are solved against different ranges, so nothing structural keeps them in
order, and crossing them would start the first sentence inside a device that is still a slot.

### The sequence, and why every silence is a frame of its own

`screenplay` in `timeline.ts` resolves the durations into the faces the screen shows. Measured against
authored, every landmark inside 21ms:

| | authored | measured |
|---|---|---|
| the object stands, naming itself | 0 | 0 |
| "We don't build websites." | 900 | 921 |
| silence | 3620 | 3641 |
| "We create digital experiences…" | 4660 | 4680 |
| silence | 8580 | 8601 |
| the work | 9740 | 9761 |

`hush` is a real step with nothing on it, and that is the point: a line leaving as the next arrives is a
dissolve, and what was authored is a *pause*. Verified across 1513 consecutive animation frames — the two
sentences were never both on screen, not once.

### The fetch happens a step early, and the loading state is usually never seen

`asksAt` is derived, not authored: the document is requested when the **second sentence begins**, which
gives it the longest hold in the sequence to arrive in. So `loading` is a frame that flashes or never
appears — and when the network is slow it is a real wait rather than a decorative one. Nothing is faked
either way; the state lasts exactly as long as the document takes. Measured on a warm cache, the sequence
goes straight from the second silence to the running experience.

The `<iframe>` still does not exist until then, and it was verified across the same 1513 frames that the
prefetched document is never visible before the sequence reaches it.

### The state model, and the two defects that shaped it

The rule the brief asked for is *no half-finished sequence appearing unexpectedly after reversing scroll*.
The model is three lines:

- inside the stretch → walk
- outside it, mid-sequence → stop, clear, back to the first step
- **reached the end → stay there, whatever the scroll does afterwards**

That last line is not a shortcut. Rewinding a manifesto the visitor has already sat through because they
scrolled up to read the claim again would be a worse failure than the one the reset prevents, and it would
re-fetch a remote application to do it.

**Defect one: the region was a viewport too long.** The sentinel was `top: --speaks-at; bottom: 0` of
`.act` — but `.act` is a viewport taller than `--act-pin`, because the frame it holds is a viewport tall.
Measured: leaving the act downward left the object still speaking to nobody three quarters of a viewport
below the visitor, and coming back found it mid-sentence. Its height is now
`--act-pin − --speaks-at`, which is exactly where the sticky frame's travel ends.

**Defect two: the object spoke through Chapter I.** A cold load of `/#work` puts the visitor at the
handover before the film has played a frame, so the object said both sentences and fetched a remote
application while the hero was still on screen. **The opening is mandatory, and that applies to the object
too** — `experience.tsx` now waits for `data-opening` to leave `running`, watched the same way
`scroll-stage.tsx` watches it. Traced from a parent frame: silent for the whole 2.4s of a hurried opening,
then the full schedule from the instant it was released.

Both were found by driving it, not by reading it.

### The state is derived, not stored

Every input is set by something outside React — an observer, a timer, a load event — and which face shows
is a pure function of them. An `outcome` held in state beside them would be a fourth thing that has to
agree with the other three, and the frames where it did not would be exactly the half-finished states this
construction exists to prevent. The lint rule that forbids `setState` in an effect body caught the first
version, which had stored it; the rule was right.

### What it bought, beyond the cinema

**The object is now most of the frame.** With no sentence standing in the paper above it, the ceiling that
kept it to 58 hundredths of the frame is gone — it is 76 wide, 78 on a tablet, 82 on a phone. Measured at
1200 × 900 the device is 320 × 680; at 390 × 844 it is 283 × 602, seventy-three per cent of the width. The
brief asked for enough presence to be a real visual anchor, and moving the words is what paid for it.

**The landscape recomposition disappeared.** §49 needed a whole branch to stand the sentences beside the
object on a phone held sideways, because 269 points of stage could not hold both. There is one thing in
that frame now, and the branch is four declarations of sizing.

### What it costs, and it is real

**The visitor can out-scroll the voice.** The sequence takes 9.7 seconds and the act's own beats end at
4.96 of 8, leaving 3.04 beats — about 167vh — of held frame past the last thing the room does. Someone
scrolling steadily through Chapter III will pass the end of the pinned frame before the second sentence
finishes, and the reset will take it back to the beginning. That is the honest consequence of two clocks:
they cannot be synchronised without turning one back into the other. The tuning knobs are the holds and
`ACT_BEATS`, and the trade is stated rather than hidden.

**Nothing here honours `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Chapter I answers that with a faster clock; this
sequence is opacity only and has no equivalent, so it runs at one speed. Consistent with `navHover` and

### A pre-existing defect this exposed, and did not cause

## 49 — The work becomes the work: one object, running, in a room that goes dark around it

Chapter III's act no longer shows footage *of* a project. It shows the project, running, on a device
standing in the frame, and the visitor can use it.

§46 built the act around two video plates: an aperture opened on a cut of stand-in footage, the frame
filled with it, the dark came up *through* the image, the two sentences were spoken inside that dark, and
a second cut returned out of it with the way in beneath. That was the right shape for a chapter whose
work was a picture. It is the wrong shape for a chapter whose work is an *experience*, and it was
carrying two disclosures it could never discharge: the footage was a stand-in for the project (§46, and
entry 41 before it), and the way in went nowhere (`work.opens: null`).

Both are discharged here. `site.three.work.url` is the production deployment, the device loads it on the
visitor's own click, and what is inside the frame is the thing the studio shipped.

### The choreography, and the one thing that changed about its logic

Two beats are gone (`plateFills` merged into the object's own aperture, `secondVisual` deleted), two are
new (`object`, `screen`), and one is new-but-not-a-beat (`atmosphere`). The act is 6.35 beats against
7.25, inside an unchanged `ACT_BEATS` of 8 — so the runway past the last beat grew from 0.75 beats to
1.65, and that is deliberate: the frame that is held at the end is now one the visitor is *using*.

**The logic that changed is what the dark is for.** In §46 the dark was applied to the work — the image
lost its light. Here the dark is applied to everything *except* the work: `.act-dark` sits beneath the
object, so what the beat removes is the room and what it leaves is a lit screen. That single reordering
is what makes the manifesto demonstrated rather than illustrated, because the sentences are now spoken
over an experience that is running while they are read. `secondVisual` was deleted for the same reason —
the work never leaves, so there is nothing to return.

### Nothing in the act moves, and that is a stronger claim than before

§46's act had two things travelling: an aperture growing on both axes and a second plate arriving. This
one has exactly one movement, the object's aperture, and after it closes the composition is **fixed** to
the end of the chapter. Everything from there is light, colour and language.

That was not the first design. The alternative — the object rising into the frame's centre as the claim
vacated it, which the brief offered as an option — was rejected on two grounds, one of them measurable:
a `transform: scale` or `translate` on an element containing a live cross-origin frame re-rasterizes it,
and the act is reversible by construction, so a visitor scrolling back up with the experience running
would drag a re-rasterizing document with them. The other ground is `04-visual-language.md` §7: the
object becoming the subject of the frame is a change of *light*, not of position, and it reads as one.

**The aperture is the film's own gesture turned ninety degrees.** Chapter II's band opened sideways from
its centre because it was a landscape; this opens vertically from its centre because it is a portrait
object. Same mechanism, same curve, same reversibility.

### The screen wakes as a separate beat, and the frame that proved it

`screen` was welded to `object` for the first pass, and the reveal was wrong in a way only a screenshot
showed: the aperture opens from the object's centre and the invitation is centred on the glass, so at a
fifth of the way open the visitor was reading *Explore experience* inside a slot that was not yet a
device — a label floating in the paper. The object has to be an object before it says anything.

`wakesWhenObjectIs: 0.72`, solved backwards like every other join in the act. The glass itself is dark
from the aperture's first frame, because an unlit screen is what a device is made of; what wakes is only
what is on it.

### The dusk needed two stages, and it is the same defect entry 30's cream transition had

The act inherited a solved problem the moment the ground under the dark stopped being a photograph and
became paper. **A single cross-fade from this chapter's paper to black measures rgb(127,127,124) at its
midpoint** — five per cent warm, which is neutral, and it read exactly as `.dawn`'s note says a single
cross-fade reads: a screen being dimmed rather than light leaving a room.

So `.act-warmth` leads `.act-dark`, as `.dawn-warmth` leads `.dawn`, over 0.62 of the darkening. The
midpoint now reads rgb(117,109,99).

**The first attempt at this was also wrong, and the reason is worth keeping.** Built exactly like
`.dawn-warmth` — an opaque warm tone whose *opacity* is the gesture — it did remove the grey and it also
did four fifths of the descent in the first half of the beat, because taking paper to `#3a2f26` is most
of the way to black on its own. The fix is that this layer is a **wash** (`rgba(201,169,139,0.45)`): the
alpha is in the colour, where it is a material decision, and the timing is the opacity. The wash biases
the path; the black still travels it.

### The atmosphere is the project's colour, and the room's light is not

`.act-atmosphere` is three fields of tones **sampled from the production deployment's own stylesheet** —
`#c9a26b`, `#d9a892`, `#a9834f` against its creams of `#f3eee5` and `#efe4d2` — mixed to roughly a fifth
of their strength across most of a viewport. `04-visual-language.md` §3: saturated colour arrives from
the material, and the material here is the work itself.

Keeping that separate from `.act-warmth`'s tone is deliberate. The room's own light is the film's
material and does not change when the project does; the colour *in* the room is the project's and
changes with it. One adjustable thing would have been two ideas.

**Both were gradient blobs before they were atmosphere, and the fix was measured.** A field terminating
at 68% of its own radius has an edge, and against a flat ground that edge is visible at **three per cent
opacity** — it was, in the first screenshot of the half-dark frame. Every field now falls to zero alpha
at 100% with a midpoint stop, so the falloff is long and there is no boundary anywhere in it.

`.act-spill` is the one thing in the chapter caused by the *content* rather than by the scroll: the
screen's own light reaching into the room once the experience is running. It is two nested elements for
one field, and the nesting is load-bearing — the outer opacity is scroll-driven and must never
transition, the inner is a state change and must, and multiplying them by nesting is what stops the fade
chasing the scroll. It also cannot appear in a room that still has its light on, because it is multiplied
by `--aglow`, which is true of a real screen too.

### Whether it can be framed is data, and that was tested rather than assumed

The brief asked for the iframe where embedding is permitted and a graceful fall back to a new window
where it is blocked. **There is no honest runtime test for the second condition**, and finding that out
cost two attempts.

A refused frame is not an error event. The browser cancels the navigation, commits its own error document
*at the target's origin*, and fires `load` on it — so `contentDocument` is null and reading `location`
throws `SecurityError`, which is exactly what a frame that worked perfectly reports. Verified against a
URL sending `X-Frame-Options: deny` and against this one: **indistinguishable from JavaScript.**

The first attempt read `location.href` and treated a readable `about:blank` as a refusal. That has a
worse failure: a frame fires `load` for its own blank document *before* the navigation it was given
completes, so the working production deployment reported `away` on the first of its two load events. A
site that embeds perfectly was being sent to a new window.

So `work.embeds` is a header read, written down beside the URL, with the date and what was found — this
deployment sends no `X-Frame-Options` and no `Content-Security-Policy` at all. `false` is a fully
composed path and not a degradation: the invitation on the glass becomes a link that opens the experience
in its own window on the visitor's own click, which is also the only way it *can* open, since a window a
script opens by itself is a popup. Nothing ever pretends to load something it cannot show.

`patience` survives as the backstop for a document that never arrives at all — a connection or a
deployment that has gone away — and a blank reading now means *not yet*, never *no*.

### Two things measured that the composition depends on

**The experience gets its own viewport.** The frame is laid out at `work.viewport` — 390, the width it
was drawn for — and scaled to the glass by a measured ratio, rather than being handed whatever width the
composition leaves. `04-visual-language.md` §5 is about images and applies to documents unchanged: the
experience inside the device is its author's layout at a smaller size, never a mobile-first site
stretched or a breakpoint nobody drew. At 1920 that resolves to 390 × 845 at 0.644 — an iPhone 14,
almost exactly.

**A phone has no scrollbar in its layout and a desktop iframe does.** The frame is widened by the user
agent's own scrollbar width (measured, 17px here, 0 wherever scrollbars overlay) and the glass clips it,
which puts the experience's *content* box at 390 and the scrollbar outside the screen. Before that, the
object had a grey bar down the inside of its right edge.

### What it gives up, and it is real

### The hero's navigation, and a defect it exposed

## 44 — The mark unveils the Studio while it travels

still read as two events in sequence: the mark finishes, then the Studio begins. The brief for this one is

**Traced to:** `04-visual-language.md` §7 — *motion exists to make change comprehensible*, and the change
being made comprehensible here is a single one: the film becoming the publication. Two changes narrated
one after another describe two events, which is what the last two versions did.

### The mechanism had to change, and it reverses §43's reasoning

The page's arrival is now a **beat of the shot** — `story.studioEmerges`, driven by scroll position — not
an `IntersectionObserver` and a duration.

That is a straight reversal of entry 43, which chose the observer deliberately so that a visitor who
stopped would have the page arrive *to* them. It cannot survive this brief: "the exact moment the word
begins travelling" and "60–80% visible by the time the mark lands" are both statements about scroll
position, and a duration cannot honour either — at a slow scroll it would finish long before the mark
landed, at a fast one long after. Only position can be welded to position.

What that buys, beyond the sync: **the reversal is now exact.** Every value retraces, sampled at eight
beats up and down — which the observer version could never do, because a shown-once fade cannot un-show
while the page it belongs to sits inside the film's own frame.

What it costs, stated plainly: stopping mid-travel now holds a partially-lit page instead of having it
complete on its own. That is the trade the brief asks for, and it is the same trade every other beat in
the shot already makes.

almost disappear" asks for, and it is worth being clear that this is the ceiling rather than a tuning
choice: any scroll-driven offset is spread over a scroll distance two orders of magnitude larger than
itself. The felt movement in this transition is the page rising 47vh under the visitor's hand while it
lights — the 8px is a rounding error on top of it. Zero is a legitimate value for `rise`, and if the
transform's cost ever matters (it makes the page a stacking context and a compositor layer for the sake
of those 8px) that is the number to set.

## 43 — A page arrives once, as a whole

Entry 42 put Chapter III's opening on screen at the right moment and left it opening the wrong way. The
statement arrived, then a scroll, then the plate arrived, then its copy — three things appearing in
turn, which is how a landing page opens. Chapters I and II are cinematic because nothing in them
announces itself separately; Chapter III announced itself three times.

**Traced to:** `04-visual-language.md` §7 — *motion exists to make change comprehensible, and it has no
other job* — and, in the same section, *things move the way things move: with weight, in a direction,
once.* Only one thing had changed here: the chapter had arrived. So there is one arrival, and the
elements inside it do not each get their own. Also §5 — *emptiness is an instrument rather than what is
left over*, and *scale is currency: a large moment is only large beside restrained ones*, which is the
argument for the second half of this entry.

### The model: one arrival per page, not per element and not per chapter

Chapter III is a publication, so the unit is a **page**. A page comes into existence as one thing when
the visitor turns to it, and everything on it — statement, plate, copy — is already there.

The statement and the first plate are now one `data-reveal`, so they are one page. The second showcase
already was one (its plate and its copy share a reveal), so it is the next page. That is the whole
change, and the model scales: Beats 4, 5 and 6 will each be a page, and each will arrive once.

Not one reveal for the whole chapter, which was the other candidate. It reads well at today's length
and stops meaning anything at tomorrow's — a 2000vh document revealed on a single 1100ms fade is
indistinguishable from having no arrival at all, and it would also mean nothing could ever be lazily
loaded per page again.

### What the visitor sees now

The mark lands. The page it introduces is already coming into existence — all of it, at one rate. The
plate is 23.1vh below the fold at that moment, so by the time it is reached it is simply *there*, at
full opacity, sliding up. Nothing arrives on the way down the page.

The composition was tightened to match, because one reveal is not enough on its own — the two halves
still had to look like one page:

- **Two intervals instead of one.** `--within-page` (9vh) inside a page, `--between-pages` (22vh)
  between them. One interval used everywhere groups nothing, which is why every block read as its own
  section. Nothing was widened; the inner one was tightened, and that is what makes the outer one mean
  something. Measured 88px against 217.3px on desktop, 76px against 185.8px on a phone.
- **A shared top line.** Inside the opening page the copy starts where the plate starts instead of being
  centred against it. Centred, the left column read statement — long silence — copy, and the copy
  floated. Now there is one horizontal line at the head of the plate, and the gap from the statement's
  last line to the copy's first is `--within-page` exactly: one line, one interval, one page. The
  showcase further down keeps its centring, because it is two elements in a row and centring is what
  makes them a row.

### The movement was measured and declined

The direction allowed 8–12px. It is not there, and the reason is a number rather than a preference.

There is one curve, so a translate would share the fade's curve and duration — which means the offset
is spent in exact proportion to the opacity. Measured, on a 10px translate:

```
opacity   px still to travel
0.003     4.34
0.026     3.72
0.074     2.57
0.154     1.54
0.261     0.86
0.322     0.63
```

Nine of the ten pixels are gone before the element reaches 8% opacity. By the point it is legible on
paper it has 0.86px left. The movement happens in the dark: it would cost the rule that `globals.css`
opens with — nothing travels — and buy something unobservable. To be *seen* it would have to be slower
than the fade or on a second curve, and a second curve needs a line in a locked document.

There is also a structural reason. With one reveal per page, the fade runs while only the top of the
page is on screen — so a translate would move the statement and nothing else, which is precisely the
fade-up headline the direction asked to avoid, and it contradicts *already there and simply revealed*.

### The threshold was hiding a coupling

`studioBlocks.threshold` was `0.01` and is now `0`. A ratio threshold is a fraction of the *block's own
area*, so a taller block has to travel further past the line before it counts as arriving: making the
opening a whole page slid its trigger 1.4vh later and put it 0.025 beats out of step with the overlap
derived from the same number. At zero the trigger is the top edge crossing the line and nothing else,
whatever the block turns out to be. Measured after: the reveal fires at beat **6.732–6.736** at every
viewport and both pins, against 6.73 intended, and it no longer depends on the block's height.

### What is deliberately not done

**The statement and the plate are not in one frame.** A magazine opening spread — statement upper-left,
plate lower-right, both at once — is the strongest version of "one composition" and it is not allowed
here: `05-storyboard.md` Beat 2 requires the positioning sentence to be *alone on the screen, at a size
that makes it the only thing there* and explicitly forbids it being softened by *a second sentence
explaining the first*, which is exactly what "We don't build websites. We create digital experiences
that become part of the memory itself." is. So this is one composition read **down** the page: the
statement holds its own frame, and the page continues. If that trade should go the other way, it is a
change to a locked document and not an implementation decision.

## 42 — Chapter III begins inside the film's last frame

### What was measured first

### The fix is spatial, and it is one number

**Chapter III now starts inside the film's last frame rather than beneath it.** `--three-overlap` is a
negative top margin on `.chapter-three`, and its top padding is gone — the opening's distance from the

the viewport, and the observer fires when a block's top reaches `(1 − arrivesShortOf) · 100vh`. Equate
them and the overlap falls out — 27.1vh on a wheel, 34.7vh on a thumb, from one declaration that reads

Measured: `.three-opening`'s top crosses the observer's line at beat **6.734** at every viewport
tested, against 6.73 intended. Nothing about the shot's timing moved, and `--dusk` still reads
**0.8053** at beat 0.46 — the value recorded in entry 39.

**`arrivesShortOf: 0.12` replaced the `rootMargin` string.** The observer's threshold and the overlap
are two numbers that have to agree, so one of them is authored and `timeline.ts` builds the string.

### Why the observer keeps the arrival, and does not become a beat

A scroll-driven fade would put a guaranteed opacity on the screen at the handover, which is tempting,
and it is the wrong instrument. The complaint was *the visitor has to keep scrolling before anything
appears*. With a duration, a visitor who stops at the handover has the statement arrive **to** them; a
scroll-driven one would freeze at whatever fraction they stopped at and ask them to keep working for
it. `02-motion-system.md` is also explicit that Chapter III does not perform, and a flick past a
scroll-driven fade would make it snap rather than fade.

So the handover frame is: the mark in the corner, the statement's first lines low in the frame at
**9–14%** and rising on their own. Deliberate wheel pace, frozen and photographed at four viewports.

### The overlap is invisible, and that is measurable

### A third defect, found by hand and not by arithmetic

The overlap measured perfectly and then failed completely for a visitor who scrolled during the
opening — which is most of them. Wheeling hard on arrival and then carrying on gave `--origin: 1974px`
while the shot's real origin was 1111px, so Chapter III sat **87vh lower than the beat it is placed
against** and the dead moment came straight back, in full.

`--origin` is rounded *up* to whole viewports, and the reason is good: it is the one value that changes
the document's height, so writing it costs a layout of the whole page, and the moment it is written most
often is a hard flick. But the film's height is also *where the page below it begins*, so a value that
can overshoot by nearly a viewport cannot be what anything positional is measured from.

**It is now rounded while it is still moving and written exactly the moment the origin is fixed.** That
is one extra layout, on a value that was already being written, at the instant it stops changing — and
it is *more* correct about the guarantee it exists for, not less: at `scrollY === origin` there is then
precisely one `--pin` left beneath, rather than up to one more. Measured after the fix: origin
1112.22px exact, the crossing beat 6.737 — identical to the untouched case — and 3872.9px of pin left
beneath a 3869px pin.

Worth recording as a method note, not just a fix. Every number in the first pass was right, and the
feature did not work. `01-validation.md` says to drive it by hand; this is what that gate is for.

### The phone had a second defect, and the overlap exposed it

On a phone the marker is `absolute` rather than `fixed` — entry 41, correctly: there is no spare margin

**It still only lives for about 8vh of scroll before the page carries it off.** That is entry 41's

### One residual, honestly

browser whose toolbar makes `dvh` smaller than `vh` that disagreement is **percentage points, not
pixels** — which would leave real dead pinned scroll at the end of the shot on a phone. It could not be
tested here and was not changed here: making the driver measure a beat the way CSS measures a length
retimes the whole shot's mapping and needs its own validation pass. Recorded so it is not rediscovered.

the word is *for*. It stops being the end of a sentence and becomes an orientation mark. Nothing else
moves because nothing else changes purpose.

the reduction comes out at 0.196 on desktop, 0.312 on tablet and 0.458 on a phone, gentler on small

own top-left, and the translate then places that corner exactly on the anchor.

## 35 — Responsive validation across four viewports

Measured at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 768×1024 and 390×844. Four real defects, no cosmetic ones.

### Touch gets a longer runway, not a shorter one

The worst finding, and counterintuitive. A wheel notch moves about 100px, so 140vh is roughly
fifteen deliberate steps on a desktop. A flick on a touch screen carries 800 to 2000px of momentum —
against a 1182px shot on a phone, **the entire dissolve, marker and statement could pass in a single
thumb movement.** The narrative would simply not be seen.

`--pin` is now 210vh under `@media (pointer: coarse)` — keyed to the pointer rather than to width,
because a large tablet has the same thumb. The script divides its beat map by `--pin`, so this is the
whole change: every ratio inside the shot is untouched and only the distance differs. Verified by
running the full beat sequence at both values and comparing: **largest difference 0.0019**, at the
steepest point of the dusk ramp, which is sub-pixel scroll rounding rather than divergence.

`--pin` is also re-read on resize now. It was captured once at mount, so a tablet rotated across the
media-query boundary would have left the timeline and the pinned height disagreeing — the one way
this can visibly break.

### Tap targets

The nav words were **19px tall and 40px wide on every viewport** — under the 24px WCAG minimum and
nowhere near the 44px touch guideline. Now 45px, via vertical-only padding so the horizontal rhythm
is untouched, with `.ways` subtracting the same amount from its own offset. Measured: the type sits
at 48 / 40 / 46 / 38px from the bottom, exactly where it sat before.

### Safe areas

`viewportFit: 'cover'` added, which is what makes `env(safe-area-inset-*)` report real numbers at all
— without it the values are zero and any padding written against them silently does nothing. The nav
now clears the home indicator, and the cards take the larger of their 6vw margin or the side inset,
which matters in landscape on a notched phone.

### Typography scale

The marker was **16.6px at 1440 and 22px at 1920** — the same chapter card reading differently on two
ordinary desktops, because its `1.15vw` did not reach the cap until 1913px while the statement capped
at 1217px. Now `1.6vw`, so it reaches full size by laptop width. Phone and tablet unchanged at 15px.

### Everything that was already correct

No horizontal overflow at any size. No gaps on either axis — the footage covers at 11% / 20% / 63% /
77% horizontal crop, and the figure survives the crop at every one. The statement holds its two
authored lines everywhere. The tagline holds one line everywhere. Marker and statement land on the
title's exact axis at all four sizes: offset 0.

**Performance:** scrolling the whole shot on a phone-sized viewport, median frame 6.9ms, p95 7.1ms,
worst 7.3ms, **zero frames over 16.7ms.**

### Not verified here

`pointer: coarse` cannot fire in an iframe, so the media query itself was proven by forcing `--pin`

## 34 — Every visit begins at the beginning

Closes the item flagged open in entries 26 and 27. The homepage is a sequence, not a document, so
it no longer restores a scroll position.

**Two mechanisms, because one does not cover it.**

`history.scrollRestoration = 'manual'` plus a `scrollTo(0, 0)`, in a synchronous inline script that
is the first thing in the body. Early placement is doing two jobs at once:

- It runs before the browser reaches the point where it would restore, so the restore never happens
  rather than happening and being corrected. Measured: `scrollY` is already 0 on the first read
  after a refresh, so there is no frame of Chapter II to see.
- **It runs before any listener exists, and that matters more than it looks.** The hero treats a
  scroll event as the visitor asking to move on and accelerates the remaining sequence. A reset
  performed after hydration would have fired that event and rushed the opening for somebody who had
  not touched anything. Verified after a refresh from deep in Chapter II: `data-hasted` is `false`,
  so the opening plays at its intended pace.

A `pageshow` handler covers the one case the script cannot: a page reinstated from the back/forward
cache is restored whole, scroll position included, and `scrollRestoration` has no say over it. The
handler only acts when `event.persisted` is true, so ordinary loads are untouched.

**Verified.** Three consecutive refreshes from 681px each returned to 0 immediately and stayed
there. A simulated bfcache restore from 800px returned to 0. A non-persisted `pageshow` correctly
left scroll alone. Hero type, dark, marker and statement all read as the first frame afterwards,
and the footage restarts.

**Not verified: Safari.** Only Chrome is available here. `history.scrollRestoration` and the
`pageshow`/`persisted` pair are both long-standing in Safari, and Safari relies on bfcache more
heavily than Chrome, which is precisely why the second mechanism exists — but it wants a real
device before anyone calls it done.

Nothing in this shot is timed in seconds. Every value is a pure function of scroll position, so

Verified: at every one of thirteen beats the four values are identical to what they were before,
simply reached sooner.

### The held beats, measured

41% of the shot is deliberately static — the bridge, the marker alone, the wait, the statement's

## 30 — The intertitle, and why three passes at it failed

The numeral is the chapter title. Three previous attempts — 15px, 24px, 36px — each raised a
number and each stayed an annotation. The reason they failed is worth writing down, because it was
a reasoning error rather than a taste one.

**Presence is ink, not point size.** "Chapter One" at 96px lays down roughly 3,800px² across
eleven glyphs. *II* is two narrow strokes: at 36px it had about a tenth of that, and even set
point-for-point with the title it would still have read as subordinate. Two strokes have to be
very much larger to carry the same authority.

**208px** — `clamp(4.5rem, 11vw, 13rem)`, 2.17× the title, 21% of the viewport's height. Measured
centre offset from where the title sits: **(0, 0)**. Everything else is inherited rather than
invented — the family's axis, the position, the 0.62 brightness. Only the scale is new, which is
what was asked: presence through scale, not through brightness or effect.

### The serif, which is not a brand decision

At intertitle scale *II* in the system sans is **two bare rectangles**. It stops reading as a
numeral, and over a video it reads uncomfortably close to a pause glyph. Tightening the tracking
was tried and cannot fix it: closer bars are still bars. What makes a Roman numeral read as one is
its terminals — the serifs are the difference between two strokes and two letters.

So the numeral, and only the numeral, is set in a serif stack under `--numeral-face`. This is a
property of the character rather than a choice about the studio's voice, and it is scoped to a
single element. When `open-decisions.md` §7 is answered the numeral should take whatever display
face is chosen — and if that face is a sans, this problem returns and wants a companion serif for
numerals, which is a normal thing for an identity to have.

Recorded as evidence for §7: this is the first time the undecided typeface has actively blocked a
design rather than merely deferred one.

**A collision caught on the way:** `--numeral` was already the card's opacity variable, so
defining a font stack under the same name silently broke the reveal. The stack is
`--numeral-face`.

## 26 — EXPERIMENT: the dissolve from Chapter I into Chapter II

Not approved, and not locked. Built to be judged, and removable in one commit — the hero itself
was not touched.

**The hero is pinned, not scrolled away.** `.chapter-one` is 100dvh plus a 55vh dissolve zone,
and the hero is `position: sticky` inside it. So the image does not slide: it stays exactly
where it is and the light goes out of it. `04-visual-language.md` §2 — a ground change is the
slowest thing in the system and nothing else moves during one.

**Scroll is the only input.** The footage keeps looping underneath, never paused, never sought,
its timeline given no say in anything. Three eased numbers are published to the document and CSS
does the rest: `--dusk` the dark coming up, `--veil` how much of the hero's own type is left,
`--two` how present Chapter II is. Each is smoothstepped rather than linear — a straight ramp
reads as a fade being performed, an eased one reads as a light going down.

**Words leave before the light does.** The veil is gone by 42% of the zone while the dark is only
at 0.43, so there is a stretch where the photograph is alone again with nothing written on it,
and only then does it go. That gap is the part that makes it feel like one chapter becoming the
next rather than two sections meeting.

**There is no seam to find.** The dark the dissolve arrives at and the black Chapter II is made
of are the same colour, so when the hero finally unpins and slides away there is nothing to see
it happen against. What ends Chapter I is that the image is no longer underneath.

Two things that had to be got right:

- **`overflow-x: hidden` on the root would have silently broken all of this.** `hidden` makes the
  root a scroll container, and a scroll container defeats `position: sticky` in every descendant.
  It is now `overflow-x: clip`, which trims the bleed without becoming scrollable.
- **The hero's type is wrapped in one layer** so the dissolve can take it away without touching
  the opening sequence's own reveal. The two multiply rather than fight: the sequence decides
  whether a thing has arrived, the dissolve decides how much of the hero is left. Scrolling in
  the first second therefore cannot leave the title fading in and out at the same time.

Measured, and reversible by construction because every value is a pure function of scroll
position: slow stepping, a single jump to 3vh, stopping at 0.28, and returning to the top all
give the expected numbers, and the top always restores exactly `dusk 0 / veil 1`. The video
reported `playing` with `loop=true` at every sample.

Chapter II's arrival is skipped for anyone who asked for less motion — the text is simply

## 21 — Interaction accelerates the sequence; it never completes it

**Supersedes decision 17.** Interaction no longer jumps to the end state. The visitor who
wants to move on gets the same choreography, sooner — every step, in order.

**The clock changed, not the schedule.** The sequence now runs on its own clock advanced by a
single animation frame loop, and interaction changes that clock's *rate* rather than its
contents. Every interval between steps keeps its proportion, so the order cannot be broken,
shortened or skipped by construction — there is no code path that reveals anything out of
turn, because there is no code path that sets a beat other than the next one.

Every duration in the CSS is multiplied by `--haste`, and the script advances its clock by
exactly the reciprocal. So the fades keep their relationship to each other *and* to the gaps
between them. One number sets the pace of the whole opening.

The same mechanism replaced the reduced-motion branch. Reduced motion is now the identical
choreography on a faster clock rather than a separate set of durations, which is what
`03-design-principles.md` asks for — a composed version, never the same thing with parts
removed.

**1.55×, not 1.8×.** Hurrying also releases the footage gate on the line, and that removes
about 750ms of waiting on top of the faster clock. At 1.8 the compound effect reached **2.3×**
for a visitor interacting late — outside the intended range. At 1.55 the observed speed-up
measures 1.75× · 1.78× · 1.85× · 2.00× for interaction at 300ms, 1200ms, 2600ms and 4000ms.

**Why the footage gate is released when hurrying.** At normal pace it holds the line back
until the second shot, which is the approved choreography. Waiting on the video while the

## 28 — Every timing moves to one module, and the curve stays alone

*6 August 2026. An architectural change with no visual consequence, recorded because two of the
decisions inside it are design decisions and will be argued about again.*

All motion values now live in `src/motion` — the duration ladder, the opening's cue points, Chapter
II's beat map, Chapter III's observer thresholds, the curve. No component holds a timing and
`globals.css` declares none; it reads them from a stylesheet generated by the module. The standard is
`docs/development/02-motion-system.md`.

**Traced to** `01-vision.md` — feeling is structure, and it is built or it is missing. Rhythm that
cannot be edited in one place cannot be tuned, only disturbed: you find four of the five numbers,
change them, and the fifth quietly disagrees. Also `CLAUDE.md`, that comments record what was
measured because the numbers are the argument — putting the numbers together puts the argument
together.

Three decisions inside it that are not mechanical:

**The curve stays alone.** The obvious shape for a motion system is a ladder of easings —
`SMOOTH`, `SOFT`, `CINEMATIC`. This system has one CSS curve and refuses the rest, because
`04-visual-language.md` §7 says motion exists to make change comprehensible and a curve with
character of its own competes with the thing it describes. Offering curves nothing is allowed to use
would turn a ban into a trap for whoever reads the module next. **Adding a curve now requires a line
in a locked document and an entry here.** The second easing that does exist — smoothstep, for
scroll — is a different mechanism, not a second voice: a scroll-driven value has no duration, so its
easing has to be a function of progress callable at any position, in any order, backwards included.

**No empty tokens.** There are no blur, scale or move duration tokens, because nothing blurs (§7,
and `globals.css` forbids it outright) and exactly one element transforms — the word `chapter`
travelling into the corner, which is scroll-driven and therefore has a range and no duration. There
is no stagger helper, because Chapter II's cadence is four unequal holds — long, short, short,
medium — and a stagger helper would make that rhythm expressible only as a metronome, which is the
thing it was composed to avoid. An empty token is not neutral; it is an invitation.

**Two things sit outside `--haste` on purpose.** Chapter III's arrival and the navigation hover are
not multiplied by it. The film is over by Chapter III, so there is no sequence left to hurry; and an
interface that answered at a different speed depending on how somebody scrolled two minutes earlier
would be responding to the wrong thing. This was already the behaviour, by accident, because both
were hardcoded. It is now the behaviour on purpose.

**`--haste` is why the system is shaped this way.** It was four literals in a stylesheet kept in step
with two constants in a component by a comment asking whoever edited one to remember the other.
Nothing enforced it, and the symptom of getting it wrong would have been fades overlapping slightly
wrongly — visible, and almost impossible to attribute. The reciprocal is now computed from one
origin. So is the shot's first frame: eighteen defaults that were maintained by hand are now the
timeline evaluated at scroll position zero. **Where two numbers must agree, one is computed.**

**Verified identical, not assumed identical.** The new timeline was swept against the old formulas at
0.001-beat resolution from −0.1 to 7.2 beats — 131,418 comparisons of the exact strings that reach
CSS — with zero differences, and again live in the browser against real scroll positions at 281
points. All 36 constants and the beat schedule match the originals exactly. The four generated
`--haste` values reproduce `1 · 0.645 · 0.45 · 0.29`. Replayed at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 768×1024 and
390×844.

## 29 — Story timings are per-beat. Nothing shares a duration.

*6 August 2026. Supersedes the shared-ladder part of §28, which was wrong, and keeps the rest.*

§28 centralised every timing into `src/motion` and, in doing so, built a shared duration ladder —
`VERY_FAST` · `FAST` · `NORMAL` · `MEDIUM` · `SLOW` · `BREATHE` · `CINEMATIC` — with each element
pointing at a rung. That is the standard shape for a motion system and it was the wrong shape for
this one.

**Every narrative beat now owns its own timing object, and no two beats share a number.**
`src/motion/story.ts` reads top to bottom as the piece: `timestamp`, `video`, `chapterOne`,
`subtitle`, `navigation`, `heroWords`, `blackTransition`, `chapterTwoMarker`,
`everyUnforgettableMoment`, `wedding`, `exhibition`, `artist`, `finalPerformance`, `creamTransition`,
`someMomentsDeserve`, `leadLeaves`, `anotherLeaves`, `chapterTravels`, `periodLeaves`, `iiiStudio`,
`studioBlocks`, `navHover`.

**Traced to** `01-validation.md`, Experience validation — the question asked of this page is what a
visitor feels *at each moment*, and whether the order still earns it. That question is asked of one
beat at a time. A shared rung answers it for seven beats at once, which is exactly the wrong
granularity: tuning how long "A wedding." resonates is not an opinion about how long the hero title
takes to fade, and a ladder makes it one.

Also `01-vision.md` — attention has to be *given*, deliberately, by somebody. A rung is the opposite
of a deliberate choice about a specific moment. It is a default that several moments inherit.

Consequences, and they are the point:

- `story.timestamp.fade` and `story.navigation.fade` are both 1000ms and are **two numbers**. They
  were one shared rung for about an hour. They are not the same 1000.
- There is no indirection between the name of a beat and the number that moves it. Nothing to follow.
- Beats that are *required* to agree still derive from one origin — the four `--haste` values, a
  beat's departure from its `hold`, the shot's eighteen first-frame values. Independence is for
  numbers that merely coincide, never for numbers that must match. Telling those two cases apart is
  the judgement the system asks for.
- Per-beat CSS properties, named for the beat: `--fade-timestamp`, `--fade-video`,
  `--fade-chapter-one`, `--fade-subtitle`, `--fade-navigation`, `--fade-studio-blocks`,
  `--fade-nav-hover`. The shared `--reveal-quiet` that fed both the timestamp and the navigation is
  gone, split in two.

**What §28 got right and this keeps:** one curve and no second one; no empty tokens for blur, scale or
stagger; Chapter III and the hover deliberately outside the haste multiplier; and shared *mechanism* —
`rise`, `fall`, `show`, the assembled track, the generated stylesheet. **Easing and mechanism are
shared and never per-beat. Timings are per-beat and never shared.** That sentence is the system.

## 30 — Anchors are absolute. Everything else states a relationship.

*6 August 2026. Extends §29; supersedes nothing.*

§29 gave every narrative beat its own timing object. Those objects still held absolute times —
`chapterOne.at = 3100`, `subtitle.at = 4900`, `wedding.at = 2.60` — which meant editing the pacing
still required arithmetic, and moving one beat quietly invalidated the ones after it.

**A beat now states its relationship to the beat before it. Only anchors carry a coordinate.**

```
subtitle:   { afterChapterOne: 1800, … }      not  { at: 4900 }
navigation: { afterSubtitle: 1800, … }
wedding:    { after: 0.10, hold: 0.11, … }    not  { at: 2.60 }
exhibition: { after: 0.05, hold: 0.02, … }    not  { at: 2.94 }
```

`src/motion/timeline.ts` resolves the storyboard into the absolute values the sequencer and the driver
need. Nothing in the project writes an absolute time down.

**Traced to** `01-validation.md`, Motion validation — the thing to review is timing, rhythm and the
distance a sequence occupies, and all three are *intervals*. A pause is a gap between two things, not a
coordinate. Also `03-design-principles.md` §2, we decide the order: an order is a sequence of
relationships, and expressing it as a list of coordinates is a lossy encoding of the thing we actually
decided.

The anchors, and why each one is one:

- `timestamp.at`, `video.at` — the first two things that happen. Nothing precedes them.
- `chapterOne.at` — the moment the whole sequence is built around. Everything else in Chapter I is
  positioned so that this lands where it lands.
- `heroWords`, `blackTransition`, `chapterTwoMarker` — the shot opens on scroll position zero, and the
  marker is the fixed point the rest of the shot hangs from.

Consequences:

- **Ripple edits.** `wedding.hold` 0.11 → 0.25 moves the other three occasions, the cream transition,
  Act III and the marker, and preserves every gap: 0.05 · 0.02 · 0.04 · 0.08 before and after.
- **The overlap trap in §29 is gone.** A hold can no longer push one occasion into the next, because
  the next is chained to its departure. The constraint that `wedding.hold` had to stay under 0.16 no
  longer exists — it was an artefact of absolute positions, and it is now unrepresentable rather than
  documented.
- **`BEATS` became an assertion.** The shot's length is checked against it rather than assumed, so a
  ripple that pushes the tail past the pinned frame is a console complaint rather than a beat nobody
  ever sees.
- `after` means *after the previous beat has completely gone*, not after it arrived. That is the whole
  reason overlap becomes unrepresentable.

**Three intentions are asserted rather than trusted**, because they are true by intent and not by
construction. Each was tested by breaking it deliberately and confirming the complaint names the number
to change:

1. The timestamp has cleared the frame at the exact instant the identity arrives — "no pause between
   them". Two authored numbers approaching one instant from opposite directions: an anchor
   (`chapterOne.at`) and a hold (`timestamp.hold`). This one could not be computed away without
   demoting an anchor the brief keeps, so it is checked instead.
2. Every gap is positive. The one route back to two beats sharing a frame.
3. The shot fits inside `BEATS`.

Stripped from the production build.

## 31 — The opening is mandatory. Scrolling hurries it; it cannot skip it.

*6 August 2026. A bug fix, and the principle it turned out to depend on.*

**The bug.** A visitor who scrolled immediately on arrival went straight into Chapter II. The shot was
a pure function of `scrollY`, so a single flick put them a viewport into the dissolve before the
timestamp had appeared — hero words gone, footage dimming, the chapter marker arriving over a
photograph nobody had been given time to see. §21 gave interaction the power to *accelerate* the
sequence; nothing stopped it *arriving underneath* it.

**Traced to** `05-storyboard.md` Beat 0 and `03-design-principles.md` §2 — we decide the order, they
decide the pace. The order is ours, and an order that can be walked out of is not one. Also
`01-vision.md`: attention given in advance is most of the work, and a first frame nobody sees is that
work thrown away. And `01-validation.md`, Interaction validation, which already required that nothing
is skipped and nothing arrives out of order under fast scroll — this was that requirement failing.

**The fix, in three parts, all of them necessary.**

1. **The clock's rate follows the hand, continuously.** `pace.haste` (1.55) is now only the floor for a
   single intent. `pace.urgent` (3) is the ceiling for somebody scrolling hard, and the rate scales
   smoothly between them against measured scroll speed (`pace.urgentAt`, 3 px/ms), smoothed
   (`pace.settle`) because a wheel arrives in impulses and a phone reports nothing between momentum
   samples. `--haste` is published inline as the rate moves, so the fades keep their proportion to the
   gaps at every speed — which is what stops "faster" becoming "everything at once".

   **3, not higher.** Above about 4 the fades arrive on top of each other faster than the eye separates
   them, which is skipping by another name.

2. **A skipped beat is now unrepresentable.** `maxAdvance` in `timeline.ts` bounds the *virtual* time
   one frame may add, derived as a third of the closest gap between any two Chapter I cues — so
   consecutive beats are always at least three frames apart whatever the rate. Derived rather than
   declared, so it maintains itself when the storyboard is retimed; floored at `pace.maxStep` so it
   never binds at natural pace. `pace.maxStep` bounds the input and this bounds the output; both are
   needed, and the old one alone was never going to be enough once the clock could run fast.

3. **The shot begins where the opening ends, not at scroll position zero.** `data-opening` on the root
   says whether Chapter I is still running; the driver derives the shot from `scrollY - origin` and
   takes `origin` on the first frame after it reads `done`. `--origin` is added to the pinned height so
   the shot always keeps its full runway and the visitor can never reach the bottom mid-opening.

**Nothing is frozen and nothing is swallowed.** The page scrolls normally throughout — the shot simply
is not listening yet. That was a requirement, not an implementation detail: a page that stops
responding to the wheel has taken something away from the visitor, and §2 says the pace is theirs. What
they get instead is the sequence answering them.

**Release is on the navigation having *finished* arriving**, not on its first frame. Releasing earlier
let a visitor already scrolling hard fade the navigation out through the veil while it was still fading
in — the beat happened and they never saw it, which is the same failure wearing a different hat.

**The dead stretch, and why it collapses.** A visitor who flicks hard leaves a length of scroll above
the shot that does nothing. Scrolling back up brings the origin down with them, so it collapses behind
them — free of visual consequence, because above the origin the shot is already at its first frame and
moving the origin cannot change a value. Measured: released at 45,300px, `--origin` 45,900px; back at
the top, `--origin` 0px, and scrolling down from there enters Chapter II immediately.

## 32 — `CHAPTER II` rewrites itself into `II Philosophy`

*8 August 2026. A brief, and a distance nobody is allowed to write down.*

**What was asked.** The Chapter II marker should stop being a chapter line and become the chapter's
name, as one continuous scroll-driven gesture: the word fades and softens, the numeral sets off *while
the word is still going*, it settles, and the topic arrives beside it. Explicitly not fade → pause →
move → appear. No scroll lock, no clock, no second easing, no bounce.

**Traced to** `04-visual-language.md` §7 — motion exists to make change comprehensible. §299's argument
for the one travelling word applies here at a smaller scale and for the same reason: what changes is
what the mark is *for*. It stops numbering the chapter and starts naming it, so the word that only
numbered it leaves. That is the second element in the piece permitted to move, and the last.

**The overlap is the decision; the offset is arithmetic.** `numeralSetsOffWhenWordIs: 0.76` says the
numeral leaves when the word is three-quarters gone. It is *not* a delay: the fade is a smoothstep, so
76% faded is 68.1% of the way through the range, and `unsmoothstep` solves for it — the same trick, and
the same reason, as `litWhenTheMarkerLands` in §44. Authoring the delay instead would have meant writing
0.1635 in the storyboard and having it silently become the wrong overlap the first time the fade moved.

Measured at the midpoint of the overlap: word 0.148, blur 2.98px, numeral 0.023 — a soft ghost of
`CHAPTER` with `II` just under way. Below about 0.6 the two read as a cross-fade; at 1.0 the pause the
brief forbids comes back.

**No overshoot, and none simulated.** `--mknum` is the shared smoothstep, which has zero slope at both
ends, so the numeral arrives at rest for free. A spring would have been a second curve, which
`easings.ts` exists to refuse.

**The blur is 3.5px and rides the word's own range.** Not a separate beat — one range driving opacity
and focus together, so a retiming cannot separate them. At a 15–22px marker that is about a sixth of
the cap height: the letterforms soften and nothing is ever a blur you would name. It rises
monotonically rather than peaking at the midpoint, because reversed it should read as the word coming
*into* focus as it arrives.

**The distance is measured, and this is the part worth remembering.** `Philosophy` is far wider than
`Chapter`, by a different amount at every size, and it is set at 0.06em against the marker's 0.34em. The
brief said not to reuse the old distance blindly, and there is no distance to reuse — so
`scroll-stage.tsx` reads the live layout and derives the one offset that puts `II Philosophy` centred
exactly where `CHAPTER II` was, publishing it as `--mk`.

Both compositions are measured by their **ink**, not their boxes. Tracked-out type carries a trailing
letter-space after its last glyph, and centring the boxes centres that phantom instead — which is the
same fault `.card-marker`'s `text-indent` already corrects for the resting line, so the moved line has
to make the same subtraction or the two disagree by half a tracking unit. The reference is the resting
line's own optical centre rather than the card's, so the measurement never has to know how the card
centred anything: safe-area padding, measure and viewport all stay the stylesheet's business. Measured,
at three widths: resting centre, final centre and card centre agree to 0.1px.

`--track` is now named on `.card-marker` because three declarations have to agree about 0.34em, and one
of them is a gap measured from the far side of it. The topic's gap is `calc(0.7em - var(--track))`, so
the ink between numeral and name is 0.7em — the same gap `.mark-slot` puts between `III` and `Studio`,
because it is the same relationship one chapter earlier.

**It costs no scroll.** The whole transformation is 0.714 beats inside the marker's existing 1.0 hold,
which leaves 0.286 of stillness — a longer breath than the statement that follows gets (0.28). The shot
already ended at 6.93 of `BEATS: 7`, so there was no room to lengthen anything, and nothing downstream
moved: 15 positions swept across the full shot, every other value identical.

Three new checks, because none of it is true by construction: the overlap fraction is inside its range,
the word is *completely* gone by the time the numeral lands, and the finished mark stands still at least
as long as the statement's hold. The third is the one a ripple edit breaks silently — the transformation
is measured from the marker's own arrival, so shortening the hold shortens only the stillness and
nothing else would complain.

## 33 — The marker window grows; the gesture inside it does not

*8 August 2026. A retiming, and the reason it is three numbers rather than one.*

**What was asked.** More breathing room before `CHAPTER II` rewrites itself — explicitly *not* by raising
`chapterTwoBecomesPhilosophy.whole` on its own, and without shortening the transformation or the pause
before the statement.

**Why `whole` alone is the wrong edit, precisely.** The transformation is chained off the marker's
*arrival* (`marker.enter.to + whole`), and the marker's departure is chained off its *hold*. So `whole`
buys breathing room by spending the stillness at the far end — the 0.286 beats that §32 argued
`II Philosophy` needs before it is allowed to leave — and at `whole: 0.28` alone that stillness would
have fallen to 0.146, half of it. `hold` alone has the opposite fault: it extends the stillness and buys
no breathing room at all. **Adding the same number to both is what lengthens the window.** Both now say
so in `story.ts`, because it is not visible from either one.

Δ = 0.14: `whole` 0.14 → 0.28, `hold` 1.00 → 1.14. The gesture is untouched — every part of it is stated
as a proportion of the part before it, so `wordLeaves`, `numeralSetsOffWhenWordIs`, `numeralTravels`,
`topicAfterNumeral` and `topicFade` all keep their values and the word-to-topic span stays 0.5735 beats.
Measured, before and after: 0.573 both times.

`whole: 0.28` is the statement's hold, which is the shortest thing in the act that reads as standing
still. At 0.14 the mark had about one wheel notch between finishing its arrival and beginning to leave,
which read as the transformation being the point of the beat rather than something that happens to a mark
you have already read. It is now 152px of hand travel rather than 77px.

**The runway, and why 7.14.** The shot ended at 6.93 of `BEATS: 7` — 0.07 of slack — so Δ pushed the tail
to 7.07 and the fit assertion is what caught it. `BEATS` is a ceiling and raising it retimes nothing, but
it is not free either, and the reason is worth recording: `chapterThree.overlapOfPin` is
`1 − endsAt/BEATS`, so the slack between the tail and the ceiling *is* how far Chapter III reaches back
under the film's last frame. At 7.14 that reach-back stays 0.009804 of `pin` against 0.01 before — 0.08vh,
nothing. At a rounder 7.25 it would have become 0.0248 and pulled Chapter III about 6vh further up into a
frame this brief did not ask to recompose. A snug ceiling, and the assertion is what makes snug safe.

The one real cost: the shot's beats share a fixed runway, so each now costs 392/7.14 rather than 392/7
viewport-hundredths. Two per cent less, across everything. That is what buys the longer window out of the
whole film instead of asking the visitor to scroll further for it, and `pin` is untouched — total scroll
is unchanged.
