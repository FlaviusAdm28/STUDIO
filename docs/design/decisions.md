# Design decisions

*Running log. Opened 4 August 2026, at the start of the design phase.*
*Foundation is locked: `01-vision.md`, `02-positioning.md`, `03-design-principles.md`, `04-visual-language.md`, `05-storyboard.md`.*

---

Every entry carries the line it is traced to. An entry that cannot name one is not a decision, it is a preference, and it is deleted rather than argued about.

Entries are dated and never rewritten. When one turns out to be wrong it is superseded by a later entry that says so.

**How the work these entries describe is finished is not recorded here.** That is

This log holds *what* was decided and why; that document holds the standard the decision had to meet

## 01 — The ground is a location, not a theme

Neither dark nor light is the studio's resting state. `04-visual-language.md` §2 says dark is *before* and *inside*, and light is *present* and *plain*. That is a description of two places, so we treat it as one.

**The work is entered by going dark. The studio speaks in plain light.**

The evening is dark for its entire length, because we are inside it. The studio's first words arrive with the change to plain light. Everything practical — how the work happens, writing to us, anything the studio says in its own voice — lives in plain light permanently.

Consequences:
- Crossing between the two is crossing a threshold, and it stays the slowest thing that ever happens (§2).
- No inversion for effect. A ground is where somebody is standing, not a mood applied to a moment.
- Nothing offers a choice of ground. A preference control would make the two states themes, which is exactly what §2 refuses.
- Entering any project, ever, means going indoors. That holds for the second piece and the twentieth.

**Resolves** `open-decisions.md` §10, and resolves it as *both, with a rule* rather than by picking one.

## 02 — Words live in the shadow

Type never crosses the subject of a frame. It occupies the dark part of a composition, and nowhere else.

**Traced to:** `04-visual-language.md` §5 — *a frame is chosen, not inherited.* A word laid across a photograph changes a composition somebody made on purpose. Also §4 — type's expression comes from the space around it — and `03-design-principles.md` §5, we are not the subject.

The consequence is a capture requirement, not a matter of placement:

**Every frame in the evening is composed with shadow to spare.** The photographer is leaving room for the studio's voice at the moment of shooting, in camera, on the night. A frame that is beautiful edge to edge and has no darkness to give is a frame we cannot speak over — and it is cut, however good it is.

This is why the piece can hold language at all without breaking a single image.

## 03 — The studio speaks. On this piece it never reads.

`04-visual-language.md` §4 says type does two jobs, to speak and to disappear, and that recognising them as two jobs is the decision. The homepage needs only the first.

The studio's voice appears five times in two minutes and never exceeds one sentence. There is no long-form reading anywhere in the piece.

Consequences:
- The speaking face is chosen now, and chosen against the five real specimens rather than in the abstract: eleven words in shadow at the size a person speaks · eight words of somebody's actual speech · four words · three statements in plain light · one question asked of the visitor.
- The quiet face is not chosen now. It waits for real long-form material — writing about the work, a second project — because choosing it against invented paragraphs is how a face gets picked for how it looks in a specimen.

**Splits** `open-decisions.md` §7 into two decisions with different deadlines. Only the first is ours this month.

## 04 — One way out, from the first second, never announced

The evening must be leavable from any moment: `03-design-principles.md` §2 gives the pace to the visitor, and `05-storyboard.md` Part One §1 requires a fast route past the atmosphere for anyone who was sent here. But `05` Beat 1 forbids anything competing for the first look.

So the way out exists from second zero. It is singular, it sits in shadow under decision 02, and it never moves, never follows anyone down, never grows. It is the only persistent thing in the piece.

**Unresolved, and first on the design list:** its exact form. `04-visual-language.md` §10 demands it be *understood before it is used* while also being *quiet when not wanted*, and those two pull against each other harder here than anywhere else in the work. Solving that tension is the first thing we design, not the last.

## 05 — The name arrives one hundred seconds in

Nothing greets anyone. `05-storyboard.md` Beat 1 forbids it, and the treatment defers the studio's first word about itself until the ground changes.

That places the first appearance of *Chapter One Studio* after an empty room, in plain light, following an admission that nobody commissioned the thing just watched.

Consequence, and it is a hard test: **a drawn mark has to survive arriving there.** Most cannot. A mark introduces a brand; at that moment we are not introducing a brand, we are conceding something. The name is therefore set in type unless a mark can be shown to work in that specific position, in that light, after that silence.

**Reframes** `open-decisions.md` §9. The question is no longer *wordmark or type* in the abstract. It is whether any mark can hold at 1:42.

## 06 — The homepage is a room, not a story

**Supersedes the premise of the treatment,** which staked the homepage's identity on one occasion told end to end.

`04-visual-language.md` §1 is explicit: the system is a room whose job is to hold material it did not create, and the studio layer exists to be underneath. A homepage that *is* a single story is a picture. When the story is replaced, the identity goes with it — which is the failure the two-layer boundary was written to prevent.

So the homepage is built as an occasion-shaped architecture that any singular moment can pass through. Arrival, fullness, the exhale, emptiness, the turn. Which occasion fills it belongs to the project layer and changes entirely (§1's table).

This makes the homepage a stronger proof rather than a weaker one. A room that holds someone else's material without imposing on it is the thing the studio sells. Demonstrating that is worth more than demonstrating one good story.

**What this does not change:** decisions 01 through 05 all hold unaltered. None of them depended on the occasion, which is the first evidence that the studio layer was separated correctly.

**The hazard it introduces,** and the central design problem from here: specificity was doing the emotional work. Swappable architecture filled with unspecific material is the moody-atmosphere failure — indistinguishable from the studios `02-positioning.md` §6 defines us against. Therefore the architecture must be built so that **only specific material fits it.** A void requiring a sentence somebody actually said cannot be filled with a line somebody wrote. Every void is shaped to reject the generic.

## 07 — Material is specified, never stood in for

`05-storyboard.md` §12 forbids judging the design with placeholders. Decision 06 requires designing before material exists. Both hold, under one rule:

**Every place material will go is designed as a requirement, not as a filled space.** Shape, the shadow it must contain, what must be true of the moment it depicts, what it must not show. The requirement is the deliverable and it is what gets reviewed.

Nothing is signed off as composed until real material has met the requirement. A moment that looks resolved with a stand-in image in it has not been designed; it has been arranged.

## 08 — The public voice is "we"; the founder's story is "I"

Beliefs, methods and experiences are the studio's — *we*. The founding and personal decisions are the founder's — *I*. The studio is the product; the founder is responsible for it.

**Traced to** `01-vision.md`, which is written in the collective throughout and closes on *we made it ours*, and `02-positioning.md` §1, where the sentence is used verbatim in that voice.

Consequence: the admission at the turn is *we*. Any first-person line appears only where the subject is genuinely biographical, and never in the same breath as a claim about the work.

## 09 — English is the design language

One voice, one typographic system, one experience to design against. Portuguese is evaluated after Version 1 and is not designed for now. Client communication happens in whatever language the client prefers — that is a business practice, not a design constraint, and it does not enter the work.

Design consequence, and it is smaller than it appears: Portuguese needs only standard Latin diacritics, so almost any face that satisfies English satisfies it too. Choosing English-first forecloses very little, which means this decision costs us nothing later if the answer changes.

## 10 — The way out is one word, and it is the same door as the turn

Resolves the tension left open in decision 04.

A single word, **Studio**, in shadow, present from the first frame, fixed for the entire length of the piece. Never moving, never following, never growing, never joined by a second word.

Why that word rather than an escape label: *skip* would be understood instantly and would also inform the visitor that what they are watching is an obstacle. **Studio** names the destination instead of the exit, which means it does two jobs at once. For anyone who was sent here it is precisely the thing they came for. For the cold visitor it is the first quiet evidence that somebody made this on purpose — which is exactly what `05-storyboard.md` §3 says Arrival requires in order to move on.

**Traced to:** `04-visual-language.md` §10 — chrome earns permanence by being needed continuously, and `05-storyboard.md` Part One §1 requires the route past the atmosphere be reachable at any point, so it is needed continuously and the permanence is earned by the letter of the rule. Also §10's *understood before it is used*: a word in the visitor's own language needs no learning, where a symbol would.

**And it is not a separate mechanism.** Using it early performs the same threshold crossing as the turn: the room goes, plain light arrives. There is exactly one transition in the entire system, whether it is reached at one hundred seconds or at nine. Leaving early is not an escape hatch added to the work — it is the same door, used sooner. The way back in is that door reversed.

**Capture requirement this creates:** one region of darkness, in the same place, in every frame of the piece. The word lives there and never moves, so the dark has to be there every time. That is now the photographer's second standing constraint alongside decision 02.

# Version 1 — the opening sequence

*Superseding note: the brief for V1 specifies its own opening — a timestamp, a reveal, the
identity, a line, then the interface. Decisions 05 and 10 above described a different
architecture (the name at one hundred seconds, the word `Studio` present from the first
frame) and do not govern V1. They are kept because the reasoning still holds for the longer
piece; they are not what was built.*

## 11 — The footage was measured, not assumed

`public/media/hero/video/header.mp4` — 2560 × 1440, exactly 16:9, 9.45s, H.264.

Measured frame by frame in the browser rather than guessed at:

- **0.00–2.50s** — a wide aerial landscape, layered ridges under heavy overcast, cool and
  desaturated. Gentle motion. Mean luminance 85. Nobody in it.
- **2.50–2.95s** — a cross-dissolve, not a cut. Luminance falls 85 → 61.7.
- **2.95–9.45s** — a figure in silhouette on a ridge, backlit by the sun, warm. Nearly
  static, frame-to-frame difference around 0.5. The lower half of the frame is absolute
  black, measuring 0.7 and 0.0 in the bottom third and bottom strip.

Every timing in the sequence is derived from those numbers. Nothing is a round figure
chosen because it sounded patient.

The footage's own arc is an empty landscape resolving into a person in the light, which is
`04-visual-language.md` §2 without anyone having to arrange it.

## 12 — The identity sits in the shadow the footage already contains

The brief says the identity is centred. Dead centre of both shots is the subject — a lit
valley, then the sun and the figure standing in it. A large title there lands on the
protagonist.

So *centred* is honoured on the horizontal axis and the identity sits at 71% of the frame
height, inside the black the footage already provides.

**Traced to** the brief's own higher instruction — the photography remains the protagonist —
and to `04-visual-language.md` §5, a frame is chosen, not inherited. Decision 02 said words
live in the shadow; this footage supplies the shadow, so nothing had to be dimmed to make
room for language. No scrim, no gradient, no overlay anywhere in the sequence.

## 13 — The frame is a real box of the footage's ratio

The opening is not a full-bleed background. A box of 16:9 is fitted inside the viewport and
the type is positioned relative to *it*, so the words are composed into the photograph
rather than laid over the screen, and the image is never reshaped to fit.
`04-visual-language.md` §5.

Whatever darkness is left around the frame is not a defect. It is the room the frame hangs
in, and on a wide display it is where the interface lives.

**The exception, taken once:** in portrait a 16:9 strip in the middle of a tall screen is a
leftover rather than a composition, so the footage is cropped there. The brief permits a
crop "if absolutely necessary" and this is the only place it is taken. It is a stopgap:
`05-storyboard.md` §9 is owed a separately composed vertical frame and has not been given
one.

## 14 — The identity arrives on the dissolve

First attempt had the identity land at 3.8s and the footage roll at 4.6s, which put the
title over textured hillside for two and a half seconds before the dissolve rescued it. It
was legible and it was fighting the image.

Now the identity begins arriving at 5.3s and the footage dissolves at 6.1s, so the name is
still faint while the ground is busy and reaches full strength as the ground goes black.
The room's light falls as the name comes up.

Measured on the running page: timestamp 386ms · light 1889ms · footage rolls 3600ms ·
identity 5282ms · line 7076ms at footage 3.49s · interface 8482ms.

## 15 — The line is gated, and the footage is the guard

The line is admitted only when the identity has settled *and* the footage is past its
dissolve. Under normal playback the gate is what admits it; the footage check exists so
that a late or stalled start can never put the line on the first shot.

Honest note: the first version claimed the footage triggered the line. It did not — the
gate bound first. The comment was wrong before the code was.

## 16 — Opacity, one curve, and nothing else

No blur, no split text, no travel, no scale, no second curve. Every arrival in the sequence
is a change in light, which is the only change the sequence is about.
`04-visual-language.md` §7.

The footage does not loop. It ends on a figure that is already almost still, and returning
to a cold empty landscape would undo the arc. It plays once and holds.
`04-visual-language.md` §6 — if a still does the same work, it is the still.

## 17 — Leaving early lands where the composition is

Any intent — wheel, key, pointer, touch — collapses the sequence at 320ms rather than
removing it. It also carries the footage past the dissolve, because the end state was
composed against the second shot's black and arriving there early must not mean arriving
somewhere the type was never designed to sit.

**Traced to** `03-design-principles.md` §2, and to the condition that everyone gets a
designed version rather than the same thing with parts taken out.

The same condition drove the no-JavaScript case: without scripting the composed end state
is what arrives, because every element otherwise starts at zero opacity and a visitor with
scripting off would have been shown an entirely black page. `05-storyboard.md` §10.

## 18 — The footage changed to `hero_demo2.mov`, and every timing moved with it

Supersedes the measurements in entry 11. `header.mp4` is no longer used.

`hero_demo2.mov` — 2880 × 1440, **2:1**, 11.888s, H.264 video and AAC audio in a QuickTime
container, encoded with libavcodec.

Re-measured, because every number in the sequence derives from the file rather than from
taste:

- **0.00–3.80s** — the wide aerial landscape. Mean luminance 76, frame difference ~1.8.
- **4.00–4.40s** — a dissolve, and this time it goes *up*: luminance 76 → 88.6.
- **4.40–11.89s** — a closer, warmer framing of the figure against the sun. Luminance holds
  at ~88 with gentle motion throughout. No third shot.

What that forced:

- **The frame ratio.** 2:1, not 16:9. Left unchanged, the frame box would have cropped the
  footage — the exact thing the box exists to prevent. On a wide display the bars fall from
  180px to 73px, so the frame now nearly fills the viewport.
- **The guard.** `SECOND_SHOT_AT` 2.95 → 4.4.
- **The schedule.** The dissolve is 1.5s later in this file, which bought back the frozen
  first frame: the footage now rolls at 2400ms instead of 3600ms, so the freeze window is
  **512ms** rather than 1.71s and the image is alive as light enters it. The identity moves
  to 5400ms so it is still faint over the first shot and reaches full strength as the frame
  changes. Measured: timestamp 370 · light 1873 · footage rolls 2385 · identity 5362 at
  footage 2.99s · line 7065 at footage 4.69s · interface 8465.
- **The composition held.** Shot two is brighter than shot one, inverting the old file, so
  the assumption that the lower frame goes black had to be rechecked. It survives: by 10%
  bands the second shot reads 118, 130, 136, 143, 137, 114, 70, 25, 8, 2 from top to bottom.
  Dead centre is 143 — the brightest part of the frame, and still no place for a title. The
  identity stays at 71%, where the column through the figure falls from 46 to 17, giving
  white type roughly 13:1. No scrim was needed here either.

**The container is a delivery problem, not a design one.** Chrome reports
`canPlayType('video/quicktime')` as empty — no support — and plays the file anyway when
handed the bytes, which is why it is referenced by `src` with no `type` hint: declaring
`video/quicktime` on a `<source>` would make Chrome discard it unplayed. Safari plays MOV;
Firefox generally will not. The stream is already H.264, so remuxing to MP4 with the index
moved to the front is a container operation with no re-encode and no quality loss, and it
fixes the container risk and the trailing-`moov` round trip together. Until then, a Firefox
visitor gets the sequence with no photography in it.

## 19 — Refinement pass: the loop, and where the name arrives

Footage is now `hero_demo3.mov` — 2880 × 1440, still 2:1, 14.652s, and **three shots**:
landscape to 3.4s, a dissolve to 3.6s, the figure to 11.2s, a second dissolve to 11.4s,
then the landscape again.

**The footage is a palindrome.** Its shot boundaries are symmetrical about the middle
(14.652 − 3.5 = 11.15) and frames near the end have near-identical partners near the start.
Comparing every frame in 13.0–14.6s against every frame in 0–1.2s, the best pair is
**14.30s → 0.30s at a difference of 0.92**, against a natural adjacent-frame difference of
1.84 in the same footage. It was cut to loop.

So the loop is trimmed to that measured pair rather than wrapped at the file's ends, and it
is checked every animation frame rather than on `timeupdate` — which fires about four times
a second and would overshoot the seam by up to a quarter of a second, landing the restart
somewhere that was never measured.

Verified on the running page, capturing the post-seek frame only after it had actually
decoded: the seam runs 14.295 → 0.314 with a difference of **0.72**, below the 90th
percentile of normal frame-to-frame change (0.74), and playback advanced 0.402s in the
following 400ms — no stall. **One decoder, one seek, no crossfade.** Nothing is paid for at
runtime, which is why this was chosen over the usual two-element crossfade.

**The name now arrives inside the light rather than after it.** The reveal was lengthened to
3200ms so it is still coming up when the identity begins, and the identity moved from 5400ms
to 4100ms.

Measured, and reproducible to within 3ms across runs: timestamp 367 · light 1568 · timestamp
leaves 2066 · footage rolls 2080 · **identity 4065** · line 5867 at footage 3.80s · interface
7668.

Two consequences worth recording:

- **A full second of the photograph alone.** The timestamp has gone by 3066ms and the
  identity does not begin until 4065ms, so the image is experienced before the name.
- **The identity settles at 5666ms; the footage finishes its dissolve at 5680ms.** Fourteen
  milliseconds apart. The name reaches full strength at the exact instant the frame changes,
  which is the best moment in the sequence and is the footage's doing, not ours.

**The line is the second level of the identity, not a caption.** 19px against the title's
96px — a 1:5 ratio — brightness from 0.62 to 0.78, tracking from 0.06em to 0.045em. Grouping
does the hierarchy: 44px from the title, 62px to the interface, so the pair binds and the
interface reads as a different kind of thing.

## 20 — Refinement pass: full bleed, and the identity at the centre

Two entries above are **superseded by direction**, not by argument, and the record should say
so plainly.

**Decision 13 is withdrawn.** The frame is no longer a box of the footage's own ratio; the
footage now fills the viewport at every size and crops. That is a deliberate override of
`04-visual-language.md` §5 — *an image is never reshaped to fit the space it lands in* — and
it is the client's call, made knowing the trade. Recorded here rather than quietly dropped,
because a traceability log that only holds the decisions that survived is worthless.

Two things the sweep caught that the eye would not have:

- **`width: 100%` left an eleven pixel black strip down the right edge on every screen** —
  the scrollbar's gutter. The opening is now `100vw` and bleeds under the scrollbar, with
  `overflow-x: hidden` on the root to stop the sideways scroll that creates.
- **`100svh` is the viewport with mobile toolbars showing.** The moment they retract the
  screen grows and the hero stops reaching the bottom. Now `100dvh`, with `100vh` beneath it
  for anything that does not understand it.

Measured across 16:9, 21:9, two laptops, tablet in both orientations, two phones, square and
5:12: every one reports zero gap on both axes and no letterbox. Confirmed visually as well,
by painting each test viewport magenta so any uncovered pixel would be obvious. None was.

**Decision 12 is partly withdrawn.** The identity is now at the optical centre — the place
the timestamp occupied — rather than in the shadow at 71%. The cost is measurable and worth
writing down: behind the title the crop reads mean luminance 145, which gives white type
**3.15:1**, just past the 3:1 that large text requires, and **1.43:1 against the brightest
part of the sun's bleed**. It holds up because the middle of the word crosses the figure's
dark silhouette, but the thin strokes lose definition to the left of centre. The second level
is safer at 5.17:1.

If that ever needs recovering without moving the title, the lever is stroke weight rather
than a scrim — thin type on bright ground is what fails here, not the placement.

**The pause before the identity is gone.** It now begins at ~3050ms, the instant the
timestamp has finished leaving, and its whole fade sits inside the light's arrival, so the
name comes up with the first landscape instead of after it. A consequence: the identity now
settles at ~4650ms, before the footage's dissolve rather than exactly on it, so the
coincidence recorded in entry 19 is spent. What replaced it is better anyway — the *line* is
now admitted by the footage, arriving at exactly 3.60s of its own time, so the second level
lands with the second shot as the original brief asked.

**Entry 19's custom loop is removed, and it should be.** The file is cut to loop and native
`loop` proves it: the seam runs 14.652 → 0.015 at a difference of **0.65**, below the 90th
percentile of ordinary playback (0.80) and marginally cleaner than the 0.72 my trimmed seam
achieved. The measurement was worth keeping; the machinery was not.

## 41 — Chapter III: the studio

The film ends and a publication begins. Nothing here is pinned, nothing is driven by scroll position,
nothing performs. Blocks arrive once by being scrolled to — one opacity transition, then forgotten by
the observer so nothing re-fades on the way back up.

**The marker became architecture.** It is a fixed element now, handed over from the travelling word at
beat 6.46 by a step rather than a cross-fade: the two are pixel-identical, and overlapping two
0.65-alpha inks would darken the marker for a frame.

**It also needed a margin.** Sharing one left rule with the text read well until the page scrolled — a
fixed marker and a moving column on the same axis put "III Studio" straight through the middle of
"built for moments". `--rule` is now the marker's edge plus 7.5rem, so the marker stands in a margin
the text never enters, which is what the margin of a book is for.

**Composition.** Statement at 76px in three authored lines. Showcases at 42/58, the visual running to
the edge of the page rather than sitting in a container — measured at **55–56% of the viewport**, in
the band asked for. The second reverses and holds a still instead of moving, because a page of moving
images is a showreel rather than a publication. No cards, no borders, no frames, no device chrome.

Below 900px both stack, each keeping its own source order so the alternation survives the collapse, and
the marker stops being an overlay: there is no spare margin on a phone, so it sits at the head of the
chapter and scrolls with it. Fifteen pixels of type permanently on top of the reading column is worse
than a marker that goes by.

### Assets, honestly

There are no images in this repository — three cuts of footage and nothing else.

The moving showcase uses `hero_demo2.mov`, which was otherwise unused. **The still is a held frame of
`hero_demo4.mov`, the hero's own footage, chosen because it is already cached** — showing one image
should not cost another 36MB, which is what pointing it at the third cut would have done. Both carry
`preload="none"` and are only asked for when their block arrives; loading all three up front would put
roughly ninety megabytes in front of the first frame, which `05-storyboard.md` §11 forbids outright.

**The still wants a real photograph**, shot for the purpose and delivered as an image. A held video
frame is a stand-in and should be read as one.

### Three bugs

- **`max-width` on a padded element.** `.showcase-text` had both `max-width: 42ch` and a 192px rule,
  and with `box-sizing: border-box` the padding comes out of the max-width — leaving a 144px column
  that broke "We don't build websites." across three lines. The measure belongs on the text.
- **A cascade-order override that silently did nothing.** The narrow-screen `.marker { position:
  absolute }` sat *before* the base `.marker { position: fixed }`. Same specificity, later declaration
  wins, so the marker stayed fixed on phones. Moved after.
- **`offsetTop` changed meaning.** Making `.chapter-three` positioned turned it into the offset parent,
  so a probe reading `offsetTop` got 118 instead of 4860. That one was in my measurement, not the page —
  worth recording because it produced a convincing black screenshot that looked like a real defect.

### A correction

I attributed two renderer freezes to writing twelve CSS custom properties per frame, and optimised the
driver to write only on change. **That was the wrong diagnosis.** The tab was backgrounded
(`visibilityState: hidden`), where Chrome throttles `requestAnimationFrame` — so probes awaiting a frame
never resolved and timed out, and IntersectionObserver never fired. The optimisation is still worth
keeping, because a dozen root-level style invalidations a frame is genuinely wasteful, but it did not fix
a freeze and should not be recorded as though it had.

### Validation

Four viewports measured: statement three lines at 76/76/41/32px, lead one line, visual 55–56% and
bleeding to the page edge, reversed showcase bleeding left with its text to the right, stacking below
900px, marker `fixed` above and `absolute` below with no collision at any size, no horizontal overflow.
Act I, II and the dawn all land on their recorded values.

**Not captured: screenshots of the reversed showcase and of mobile.** The browser tab in this session
keeps losing focus, and a hidden tab is not painted — screenshots come back blank or partial. The
composition numbers above are sound; the two frames want a human eye on a focused window.

**Chapter III has no real content yet.** Copy is temporary and marked as such, and there is no portfolio,
no projects, no calls to action — as instructed.

## 40 — The marker resolves onto one line

Refinement of entry 39. Everything before the final resolution is untouched: the sentence still comes
apart the same way, `chapter.` still stands alone, and the travel is still the same 0.74-beat move.

**Stacked became inline.** `III Studio`, one horizontal line, upper-left. 15px, medium weight, slight
tracking, 76px wide in total — readable at a glance and still the quietest thing on the page.

The neat part is that this needed no new arithmetic. The numeral and the word's landing anchor now sit
in a **flex row** at the corner, so the anchor is placed after the numeral by the layout itself. The
word's landing point accounts for `III` without a single number being written down, and the survey code
that measures the travel did not change at all. `line-height: 1.25` on the slot matches the sentence's,
so the numeral's box is the same height as the landed word's and their baselines agree.

**One moment instead of two.** The numeral's arrival and the word's settle now share a single range,
6.26 → 6.46, which begins when the travel is **93% complete** — an annotation appearing beside
something almost at rest rather than a second animation starting. There is still a little movement left
to carry the swap, which is what keeps it from reading as a substitution.

**Three changes ride one cross-fade.** `chapter` → `Studio` also carries the change from light weight
to medium and from speaking ink to quiet ink, because `.mark-label` already holds the marker's weight,
tracking and colour. One change the eye can miss rather than three it cannot. The values are in `em`
so they survive being scaled with the parent and land at their intended size.

### Validation

Four viewports. The marker measures **identically at every one** — one line, 76px wide, `III` at 15px,
`Studio` rendered at 15.0px, 9px gap — which is correct for something that is page architecture rather
than content; only its distance from the corner changes, 72px down to 28px. Landing exact at all four.
Sentence holds two lines. No horizontal overflow. Act I unchanged: dusk reads 0.8045–0.8056 at the
equivalent beat on every size.

**Still open, and now more pointed:** the brief says the marker is "part of the page architecture rather
than part of the animation." It is not yet — it lives inside the pinned stage, so it will scroll away
when the stage releases. Making it genuinely persistent means a fixed-position marker and a handoff at

## 35 — Responsive validation across four viewports

Measured at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 768×1024 and 390×844. Four real defects, no cosmetic ones.

### Touch gets a longer runway, not a shorter one

The worst finding, and counterintuitive. A wheel notch moves about 100px, so 140vh is roughly
fifteen deliberate steps on a desktop. A flick on a touch screen carries 800 to 2000px of momentum —
against a 1182px shot on a phone, **the entire dissolve, marker and statement could pass in a single

steepest point of the dusk ramp, which is sub-pixel scroll rounding rather than divergence.

this can visibly break.

### Tap targets

### Typography scale

### Everything that was already correct

**Performance:** scrolling the whole shot on a phone-sized viewport, median frame 6.9ms, p95 7.1ms,

## 34 — Every visit begins at the beginning

Closes the item flagged open in entries 26 and 27. The homepage is a sequence, not a document, so
it no longer restores a scroll position.

**Two mechanisms, because one does not cover it.**

`history.scrollRestoration = 'manual'` plus a `scrollTo(0, 0)`, in a synchronous inline script that
is the first thing in the body. Early placement is doing two jobs at once:

- It runs before the browser reaches the point where it would restore, so the restore never happens
  rather than happening and being corrected. Measured: `scrollY` is already 0 on the first read
  after a refresh, so there is no frame of Chapter II to see.
- **It runs before any listener exists, and that matters more than it looks.** The hero treats a
  scroll event as the visitor asking to move on and accelerates the remaining sequence. A reset
  performed after hydration would have fired that event and rushed the opening for somebody who had
  not touched anything. Verified after a refresh from deep in Chapter II: `data-hasted` is `false`,
  so the opening plays at its intended pace.

A `pageshow` handler covers the one case the script cannot: a page reinstated from the back/forward
cache is restored whole, scroll position included, and `scrollRestoration` has no say over it. The
handler only acts when `event.persisted` is true, so ordinary loads are untouched.

**Verified.** Three consecutive refreshes from 681px each returned to 0 immediately and stayed
there. A simulated bfcache restore from 800px returned to 0. A non-persisted `pageshow` correctly
left scroll alone. Hero type, dark, marker and statement all read as the first frame afterwards,
and the footage restarts.

**Not verified: Safari.** Only Chrome is available here. `history.scrollRestoration` and the
`pageshow`/`persisted` pair are both long-standing in Safari, and Safari relies on bfcache more
heavily than Chrome, which is precisely why the second mechanism exists — but it wants a real
device before anyone calls it done.

## 30 — The intertitle, and why three passes at it failed

The numeral is the chapter title. Three previous attempts — 15px, 24px, 36px — each raised a
number and each stayed an annotation. The reason they failed is worth writing down, because it was
a reasoning error rather than a taste one.

**Presence is ink, not point size.** "Chapter One" at 96px lays down roughly 3,800px² across
eleven glyphs. *II* is two narrow strokes: at 36px it had about a tenth of that, and even set
point-for-point with the title it would still have read as subordinate. Two strokes have to be
very much larger to carry the same authority.

**208px** — `clamp(4.5rem, 11vw, 13rem)`, 2.17× the title, 21% of the viewport's height. Measured
centre offset from where the title sits: **(0, 0)**. Everything else is inherited rather than
invented — the family's axis, the position, the 0.62 brightness. Only the scale is new, which is
what was asked: presence through scale, not through brightness or effect.

### The serif, which is not a brand decision

At intertitle scale *II* in the system sans is **two bare rectangles**. It stops reading as a
numeral, and over a video it reads uncomfortably close to a pause glyph. Tightening the tracking
was tried and cannot fix it: closer bars are still bars. What makes a Roman numeral read as one is
its terminals — the serifs are the difference between two strokes and two letters.

So the numeral, and only the numeral, is set in a serif stack under `--numeral-face`. This is a
property of the character rather than a choice about the studio's voice, and it is scoped to a
single element. When `open-decisions.md` §7 is answered the numeral should take whatever display
face is chosen — and if that face is a sans, this problem returns and wants a companion serif for
numerals, which is a normal thing for an identity to have.

Recorded as evidence for §7: this is the first time the undecided typeface has actively blocked a
design rather than merely deferred one.

**A collision caught on the way:** `--numeral` was already the card's opacity variable, so
defining a font stack under the same name silently broke the reveal. The stack is
`--numeral-face`.

## 26 — EXPERIMENT: the dissolve from Chapter I into Chapter II

Not approved, and not locked. Built to be judged, and removable in one commit — the hero itself
was not touched.

**The hero is pinned, not scrolled away.** `.chapter-one` is 100dvh plus a 55vh dissolve zone,
and the hero is `position: sticky` inside it. So the image does not slide: it stays exactly
where it is and the light goes out of it. `04-visual-language.md` §2 — a ground change is the
slowest thing in the system and nothing else moves during one.

**Scroll is the only input.** The footage keeps looping underneath, never paused, never sought,
its timeline given no say in anything. Three eased numbers are published to the document and CSS
does the rest: `--dusk` the dark coming up, `--veil` how much of the hero's own type is left,
`--two` how present Chapter II is. Each is smoothstepped rather than linear — a straight ramp
reads as a fade being performed, an eased one reads as a light going down.

**Words leave before the light does.** The veil is gone by 42% of the zone while the dark is only
at 0.43, so there is a stretch where the photograph is alone again with nothing written on it,
and only then does it go. That gap is the part that makes it feel like one chapter becoming the
next rather than two sections meeting.

**There is no seam to find.** The dark the dissolve arrives at and the black Chapter II is made
of are the same colour, so when the hero finally unpins and slides away there is nothing to see
it happen against. What ends Chapter I is that the image is no longer underneath.

Two things that had to be got right:

- **`overflow-x: hidden` on the root would have silently broken all of this.** `hidden` makes the
  root a scroll container, and a scroll container defeats `position: sticky` in every descendant.
  It is now `overflow-x: clip`, which trims the bleed without becoming scrollable.
- **The hero's type is wrapped in one layer** so the dissolve can take it away without touching
  the opening sequence's own reveal. The two multiply rather than fight: the sequence decides
  whether a thing has arrived, the dissolve decides how much of the hero is left. Scrolling in
  the first second therefore cannot leave the title fading in and out at the same time.

Measured, and reversible by construction because every value is a pure function of scroll
position: slow stepping, a single jump to 3vh, stopping at 0.28, and returning to the top all
give the expected numbers, and the top always restores exactly `dusk 0 / veil 1`. The video
reported `playing` with `loop=true` at every sample.

Chapter II's arrival is skipped for anyone who asked for less motion — the text is simply
present. The dark stays, because it is a change in light locked to the visitor's own scrolling
rather than something that moves on its own.

**Fixed on sight:** the numeral was set at 13px, 0.52 alpha, 0.42em tracking, which pulled two
bare vertical strokes so far apart they read as stray marks rather than a numeral. Tracking is
for words. Now 15px at 0.62 with 0.14em.

**Flagged, not changed:** *unforgettable* is on the banned list in `04-visual-language.md` §12.
The statement was specified verbatim so it is set verbatim.

**Unresolved:** on reload the browser restores scroll position, so a visitor can land mid-dissolve
with the opening sequence playing underneath a half-dark screen. `history.scrollRestoration =
'manual'` would fix it and has not been added, because forcing the page to the top on every
refresh is a behavioural decision rather than a repair.

Also unaddressed: the boundary from Chapter II's black into the light where the studio speaks.
That is a second ground change and nothing has been designed for it.

Final hierarchy pass. Sizes only, plus one bug the pass uncovered.

Hierarchy after: title 96px at 0.92 · second level 30px at 0.78 · interface 14.4px at 0.52.

**The second level no longer wraps.** At a fixed 30px — and even at 20px — this sentence broke
to two lines on narrow screens, which stops it being a line and makes it a block. It now scales
with viewport width in portrait, `clamp(1.1875rem, 5.4vw, 1.6875rem)`, and holds one line from
360px up. A 320px screen still wraps; that is a 2016 device and two centred lines there is
graceful rather than broken.

### The bug this pass found

The virtual clock was only as reliable as the main thread. A stall — a background tab, a slow
decode, another tab taking the CPU — stops the frame loop, and the next frame arrives with a
delta of seconds. The clock leapt and several steps became due at once: caught in the act, the
light, the identity and the line all arrived **in the same millisecond**. The order held, and
three steps appearing together is still a skip to anyone watching, which is the one thing the
choreography is not allowed to do.

Each frame may now advance the clock by at most 50ms. A stall pauses the sequence instead of
fast-forwarding it, so somebody who leaves the tab and returns sees the choreography from where
it stopped. Fixing it also made the measurements reproducible, which is the tell that it was
real fragility and not noise.

### Locked state

Verified on the running page, reproducible across runs:

- Baseline reaches the interface at **6669ms**, steps separated by 1201 · 500 · 1000 · 1799 ·
  1805ms.
- Interaction at 1800ms: **scroll 4950ms, click 4954ms** — four milliseconds apart, both with
  the order intact, both at **1.55×**. Gaps 1202 · 403 · 645 · 1167 · 1166ms, so the smallest
  interval anywhere is 403ms and every step remains a separate event.
- Full bleed at every size tested, no letterbox, no uncovered pixel.
- Native loop seam 0.84 against a 90th percentile of 0.81 in ordinary playback.

**The hero is closed. Changes to it from here are regressions unless the brief says otherwise.**

The two things it still carries are not build problems and were never in scope for these passes:

## 21 — Interaction accelerates the sequence; it never completes it

**Supersedes decision 17.** Interaction no longer jumps to the end state. The visitor who
wants to move on gets the same choreography, sooner — every step, in order.

**The clock changed, not the schedule.** The sequence now runs on its own clock advanced by a
single animation frame loop, and interaction changes that clock's *rate* rather than its
contents. Every interval between steps keeps its proportion, so the order cannot be broken,
shortened or skipped by construction — there is no code path that reveals anything out of
turn, because there is no code path that sets a beat other than the next one.

Every duration in the CSS is multiplied by `--haste`, and the script advances its clock by
exactly the reciprocal. So the fades keep their relationship to each other *and* to the gaps
between them. One number sets the pace of the whole opening.

The same mechanism replaced the reduced-motion branch. Reduced motion is now the identical
choreography on a faster clock rather than a separate set of durations, which is what
`03-design-principles.md` asks for — a composed version, never the same thing with parts
removed.

**1.55×, not 1.8×.** Hurrying also releases the footage gate on the line, and that removes
about 750ms of waiting on top of the faster clock. At 1.8 the compound effect reached **2.3×**
for a visitor interacting late — outside the intended range. At 1.55 the observed speed-up
measures 1.75× · 1.78× · 1.85× · 2.00× for interaction at 300ms, 1200ms, 2600ms and 4000ms.

**Why the footage gate is released when hurrying.** At normal pace it holds the line back
until the second shot, which is the approved choreography. Waiting on the video while the
visitor has asked to move on would stall the tail of the sequence. And the measurement says
it costs nothing: with the identity now at the centre, the type is *more* legible over the
first shot than the second — title 5.41:1 against 3.15:1, line 7.46:1 against 5.17:1. The
sunset frame is the worst case for legibility, not the best, so the gate was protecting the
wrong thing.

Verified on the running page. Order correct at every interaction point, including mid-fade,
and under five interactions in a row — which do not compound, so the pace cannot be stacked
up by hammering. Baseline unchanged at 7480ms. In the earliest case the gaps between steps
measure 778 · 320 · 645 · 1160 · 1166ms: still separate events, none of them instant.
